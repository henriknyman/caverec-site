Cave Recorder (caverec)

A multitrack recorder and mixing console for bands, self-recording musicians, and studio engineers playing together in one room. One evening Session holds your mic patching and console mix while every song or jam saves as its own 00:00:00.000 Take with isolated 32-bit float WAV stems, a live stereo Print, and synchronized 50 fps multi-camera video. Includes a full Stagebox Mixer for X-Air and X32 hardware, multi-mic phase alignment, a built-in channel-strip and amp suite with .nam support, a 6-engine Groovebox (drums, monophonic bass, and Studio Sampler), dual-screen hardware pad controller support, scriptable caverec-cli automation, contextual Studio Coach guidance, wireless Cave Companion room control on iOS and Android, and open DAW and video exports.

30-Minute Rolling Memory Buffer: While stopped, Cave Recorder continuously buffers armed inputs in RAM, ready to save the jam that starts five minutes before anyone remembers to press Record.

Studio Philosophy

Built for Musicians Playing in the Same Room

An extra pair of hands at the desk • No beat grids, pitch correction, or AI generation.

Most studio software assumes one project file equals one pre-planned song built on a beat grid, recorded one overdub at a time with someone sitting at the computer. Recording a band live in a rehearsal room or project studio looks very different: microphones bleed into one another, tempo breathes with the room, loud amplifiers interact, and the person engineering the session is usually holding a guitar or sitting behind the drums.

Cave Recorder handles the repetitive desk work quietly in the background (rolling 30-minute memory capture, input trim calibration, multi-microphone polarity and sub-sample phase alignment, bleed-safe dynamics, and instant rough-mix Prints) so you can spend the evening on mic placement and playing rather than file management.

Cave Recorder omits timeline piano rolls, MIDI clip grids, tempo-warping, and automatic pitch correction. Time runs in physical seconds, milliseconds, and samples (00:00:00.000) inside independent Takes so live tempo push-and-pull stays intact.
