Multitrack Rehearsal Recording, Live Studio Tracking, Phase Alignment, and Console Mixing

Part I · Chapter 1

Overview & Studio Philosophy

Why Cave Recorder separates rehearsal Sessions into independent t=0 Takes, captures live stereo Prints, and keeps track-level Overdub Passes organized.

1.1 Why Cave Recorder Exists#

Cave Recorder (caverec) is a multitrack studio recorder, live mixing console, automatic phase-alignment processor, and band collaboration workstation purpose-built for rehearsal rooms, live tracking spaces, and project studios.

Traditional digital audio workstations are modeled around a single linear timeline designed for solo production at a desk. When a full band plugs into an 18-channel audio interface for a three-hour rehearsal or live tracking session, a single continuous timeline creates immediate studio bottlenecks: finding a specific song run-through requires scrolling through hours of waveforms, spontaneous jams are lost when nobody remembers to press Record, bandmates wait days for someone to bounce rough mixes, and multi-microphone phase cancellation hollows out the drum kit and bass rig.

Cave Recorder solves four specific studio and rehearsal problems:

1.1.1 Independent Zero-Based Takes Instead of a 3-Hour Linear Timeline#

In a band rehearsal or tracking session, musicians play multiple run-throughs of songs alongside spontaneous improvisations. In Cave Recorder, every time you press Record (R) with Overdub Mode turned off, the transport creates a brand-new, self-contained Take (Take 001, Take 002, Take 003, ...) that starts at its own zeroed timeline origin (00:00:00.000, sample 0).

Two different Takes are never appended end-to-end down a multi-hour timeline and are never stacked on top of one another in the same timeline view. Instead, switching the active Take in the Top Transport Deck (< TAKE 001 >) or via ALT + [ and ALT + ] immediately switches the Waveform Timeline and Mix Console playback to that song's 00:00:00.000 origin while keeping the room's microphone assignments, channel names, subgroup routing, and console mix intact.

1.1.2 30-Minute Retroactive Capture Memory (RAM & RETRO)#

The best moments in a rehearsal room often happen when the transport is stopped while the band is warming up or exploring a groove between songs. Whenever the audio interface is active and the green RAM indicator is lit in the Top Transport Deck, Cave Recorder continuously retains the last 30 minutes of live multi-channel audio across all record-armed channels in dedicated system memory.

Pressing the RETRO button in the Top Deck (or pressing SHIFT + R) immediately rescues the rolling 30-minute capture memory to disk as a new numbered Take (Take<NNN>), complete with individual pre-fader multitrack stems and a reference stereo file.

1.1.3 Simultaneous Live Console Print and Raw Multitrack Stems#

During every Take, Cave Recorder writes two distinct sets of audio files to disk in real time:

  1. Raw Pre-Fader Multitrack Stems (rec/): Every record-armed input channel (CH01 through CH18) writes a discrete, uncompressed 32-bit floating-point mono .wav stem tapped directly after input gain trim and prior to channel insert processing and fader moves. This guarantees clip-free archival stems for later mixing or overdubbing.
  2. Live Stereo Master Bus Print (mix/): Simultaneously, when 2-TRK is armed on the MST MASTER BUS, a dedicated 2-track recorder tapped at the post-limiter output of the Master Bus captures the live stereo console mix (Print001.wav), including all channel EQ, VCA compression, tape saturation, subgroup processing, and auxiliary reverb and delay returns.

With a single click on FLAC PRINT in the Session Reel (or automatically upon cloud upload when AUTO-FLAC ON UPLOAD is enabled), Cave Recorder encodes the live stereo Print to 24-bit .flac and links it into the project-wide _Mixes/ folder. Band members walk out of rehearsal with finished stereo reference mixes ready for phone or car playback, while the engineer retains pristine 32-bit floating-point raw stems.

1.1.4 Console-First Layout and Automatic Multi-Mic Phase Alignment#

Cave Recorder eliminates nested plugin windows and hidden routing matrices in favor of a fixed, dark-chassis studio console interface. Input gain trim, polarity inversion (Ø), per-channel phase alignment (Φ), an 8-slot reorderable processor rack (12-band parametric EQ with dual-trace spectrum analyzer, analog-modeled VCA compressor, tape saturation, and CLAP audio plugins), stereo Aux Sends (RVB and DLY), Subgroup Bus routing (SUB 1..16), and 100 mm logarithmic faders are always accessible directly on the console faceplate.

When recording a drum kit or multi-miked guitar and bass cabinets, acoustic arrival-time differences between close microphones, overheads, and room microphones cause comb filtering and low-frequency cancellation. Clicking ALIGN ALL in the Top Deck (or pressing a custom shortcut assigned in KEYMAP) analyzes the recorded multi-channel performance against your chosen reference microphone and applies sub-sample time delay (us / smp) and polarity (Ø) corrections across the console in one step.

Distinguishing Session-Level Takes from Track-Level Passes

Cave Recorder maintains a strict separation between Takes and Passes. A Take (Take 001, Take 002) is an entire song or jam recorded across the studio room starting at 00:00:00.000, selected in the Top Transport Deck or SESSION REEL (CMD + 4 / CTRL + 4). A Pass (PASS 01 Base Pass or OD 02 Overdub Pass) is an individual performance recorded on a single channel strip inside the currently active Take, displayed inside expandable Pass Lanes in WAVEFORM TIMELINE (CMD + 2 / CTRL + 2).

Hardware Setup, Audio Connections & First Session

Connecting multi-channel USB and ADAT interfaces, locking digital clocks, calibrating roundtrip loopback latency, and creating your first Project and Session.

2.1 Connecting Multi-Channel Audio Interfaces (USB, Thunderbolt & ADAT)#

Cave Recorder supports up to 18 simultaneous input channels (CH01 through CH18), making it an ideal match for rackmount 18-in / 20-out USB or Thunderbolt studio interfaces such as the Behringer UMC1820 + ADA8200, Focusrite Scarlett/Clarett 18i20 + OctoPre, RME Fireface UFX/UCX II + OctaMic, Audient iD44/EVO 16 + SP8, or Universal Audio Apollo x8p.

A standard 18-channel rehearsal and tracking rig combines three physical input groups into a single unified audio device:

  1. Inputs 1-8 (Primary Interface Onboard Mic/Line Preamps): Eight XLR/TRS combo preamps on the main rack unit, typically dedicated to close drum microphones, guitar cabinet mics, rack and floor toms, and stereo drum overheads.
  2. Inputs 9-10 (Coaxial S/PDIF or Auxiliary Line Return): Two digital coaxial S/PDIF channels or line inputs used for stereo hardware synthesizers, stage pianos, or drum machines (SpdifSynthL and SpdifSynthR).
  3. Inputs 11-18 (8-Channel ADAT Optical Preamp Expander): Eight additional microphone and instrument preamps fed from an external 8-channel ADAT expander into the primary interface's ADAT OPTICAL IN port over a TOSLINK fiber-optic cable, dedicated to BassDi, BassAmp, GuitarL, GuitarR, KeysL, KeysR, LeadVocal, and RoomAmbience.

2.1.3 Digital Clock Synchronization (INT vs. External ADAT / Word Clock)#

Whenever two or more digital audio devices convert audio simultaneously (such as a primary USB interface and an external 8-channel ADAT optical preamp), every converter in the chain must lock to a single master sample clock. If both units run on their own internal crystal oscillators, slight clock drift causes periodic clicks, pops, and high-frequency distortion across the expanded channels.

Follow one of these two studio clocking topologies:

  1. Topology A: Primary Interface as Master (INT), Expander Slaved via Word Clock or Bidirectional ADAT (Recommended):
    • Set your primary audio interface clock source to Internal (INT) at 48.0 kHz (displayed in the Cave Recorder Top Deck as 48.0 kHz | INT and in the console session bar as 48.0 kHz LOCKED).
    • Run a 75-ohm BNC Word Clock cable from the primary interface WORD CLOCK OUT to the ADAT expander WORD CLOCK IN (switching the expander's rear clock selector to WC IN with 75-ohm termination active), or run a second TOSLINK cable from the primary interface ADAT OUT to the expander ADAT IN and set the expander clock selector to ADAT IN.
  2. Topology B: ADAT Expander as Master, Primary Interface Slaved to ADAT:
    • If your external 8-channel preamp has only an ADAT OUT port and no WORD CLOCK IN or ADAT IN port, set the external preamp to INTERNAL 48 kHz and set your primary audio interface clock source to External ADAT in its hardware control panel.
Preventing Digital Clock Clicks and Sample Rate Mismatches

Standard single-wire ADAT Lightpipe carries 8 discrete audio channels at 44.1 kHz or 48.0 kHz. At 88.2 kHz or 96.0 kHz (S/MUX mode), a single optical cable carries only 4 channels. For full 18-channel band recording with an 8-channel ADAT expander, operate both hardware units and Cave Recorder at 48000 Hz (48.0 kHz) and confirm the red 48.0 kHz LOCKED badge is lit in the console header before tracking.

2.2 Configuring Audio Device Settings (AUDIO SETTINGS)#

Click the AUDIO SETTINGS button at the top right of the Top Transport Deck (or press CMD + , on macOS / CTRL + , on Windows and Linux) to open the Audio Device & Latency Settings modal (MODAL: AUDIO DEVICE & LATENCY SETTINGS).

2.2.1 Selecting Input and Output Devices#

  • Input Device: Displays all active CoreAudio (macOS), ASIO/WASAPI (Windows), or ALSA (Linux) recording devices detected on the workstation, along with their available channel counts (for example, Behringer UMC1820 + ADA8200 (18 in / 20 out) or Behringer UMC202HD 192k (2 in / 2 out)). Click the desired interface button to bind Cave Recorder's input engine.
  • Output Device: Select Same as Input whenever your studio monitors and headphone amplifiers are connected to the same multi-channel interface as your microphones. Using a single unified hardware device guarantees that input and output converters share the same sample clock and ensures deterministic roundtrip loopback calibration. Separate output devices remain selectable when monitoring through an independent desktop DAC.

2.2.2 Buffer Size (frames), Sample Rate & Bit Depth#

  • Buffer Size (frames): Selectable from 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, or 2048 sample frames per hardware buffer cycle.
    • Low Buffer Sizes (32, 64, or 128 frames): Minimize live input-to-output foldback delay when a vocalist or guitarist is monitoring their own performance through Cave Recorder's built-in console EQ, VCA compressor, and AUX 1 REVERB.
    • Medium Buffer Sizes (256 or 512 frames): Provide generous processor headroom during 18-channel full-band tracking when musicians monitor via the audio interface's direct hardware mixer, or during mixing sessions with multiple third-party CLAP plugins.
  • Sample Rate: Selectable between 44100 Hz, 48000 Hz, 88200 Hz, and 96000 Hz. Use 48000 Hz (48.0 kHz) for 18-channel USB/ADAT studio rigs and video-synchronized live sessions.
  • Bit Depth: Shows the hardware converter transport word length (24-bit (Fixed by hardware format)). Regardless of the hardware interface's 24-bit converter format, Cave Recorder processes all internal signal paths and writes all on-disk multitrack stem files (rec/*.wav) in uncompressed 32-bit floating-point format.

2.2.3 30-Minute Retroactive Capture Memory (RAM)#

While your audio interface is active, the green RAM button in the Top Transport Deck controls Cave Recorder's continuous 30-minute retroactive capture memory. When RAM is enabled:

  • Every record-armed channel strip (R engaged) continuously writes incoming 32-bit float audio into a dedicated 30-minute rolling capture memory even while the main transport is stopped.
  • At 48.0 kHz, 30 minutes of 32-bit audio requires approximately 330 MB of system memory per armed channel (roughly 5.9 GB when all 18 channels are armed simultaneously).
  • If you are working on a laptop with limited memory and do not need retroactive jam rescue, click RAM in the Top Deck to disable background capture memory.

2.3 Calibrating Roundtrip Loopback Latency (Measure Latency)#

When a musician records an Overdub Pass (Rec002-OD) while listening to playback of a Base Pass (Rec001), the backing track travels out through the digital-to-analog converter (DAC) to the musician's headphones, and the musician's new performance travels back in through the analog-to-digital converter (ADC) and USB or Thunderbolt bus.

Audio device drivers only report nominal buffer sizes (Estimated roundtrip: 13.33 ms, or 640 samples at 256 frames / 48.0 kHz). They do not account for the additional samples introduced by hardware oversampling filters, optical ADAT connections, and USB or Thunderbolt transport stages (which typically add 16 to 64 extra samples). Without hardware loopback calibration, every overdubbed track lands slightly late relative to the drums.

Cave Recorder includes a built-in hardware impulse loopback analyzer (ROUNDTRIP LOOPBACK CALIBRATION) that measures the exact physical sample delay from output jack to input jack:

  1. Lower Monitor Volume: Turn down your studio monitor controller and headphone amplifiers completely so the calibration test impulse does not play through loudspeakers.
  2. Patch a Physical Loopback Cable: Connect a balanced 1/4-inch TRS or XLR patch cable from your interface's dedicated loopback test output (Out 20 or Out 1) directly into any hardware analog input channel (such as Input 1). Ensure +48V phantom power is turned off on that input channel and set its physical preamp gain knob near unity (roughly 9 to 10 o'clock).
  3. Select the Loopback Test Output: Open AUDIO SETTINGS (CMD + , / CTRL + ,) and locate the ROUNDTRIP LOOPBACK CALIBRATION panel at the bottom. Choose Out 20 (convenient on 20-output interfaces where Output 20 can stay permanently patched to an unused line input) or click Use Out 1 if patching from main Output 1. (For acoustic room delay tests through a speaker and microphone, toggle Acoustic loopback (speaker -> microphone)).
  4. Run the Impulse Measurement: Click Measure Latency. Cave Recorder emits a short, high-precision measurement impulse out the selected output port, automatically detects which hardware input channel received the return signal, and correlates the exact sample arrival offset.
  5. Verify the Measured Offset: Inspect the green confirmation readout at the bottom of the modal and the red badge under Estimated Latency: Auto-detected Input 1 <- Output 20 | Measured: 13.83 ms (664 samples, +24 vs driver, Electrical)
  6. Close Audio Settings: Click CLOSE AUDIO SETTINGS at the top left of the modal.
How Cave Recorder Applies Your Measured Loopback Offset

Whenever you record an Overdub Pass (Rec002-OD) with OD enabled, Cave Recorder automatically tags and shifts the newly recorded Pass Lane by the negative of the total roundtrip offset (Overdub Record-Offset (G + R): -664 smp, combining any active Plugin Delay Compensation G with the calibrated hardware roundtrip R). You can inspect the exact sample offset on every Take row in SESSION REEL (CMD + 4 / CTRL + 4) and on every Overdub Pass Lane in WAVEFORM TIMELINE (CMD + 2 / CTRL + 2), or click RE-APPLY MEASURED OFFSET in the Session Reel to update earlier Takes after recalibrating.

Typical Studio & Rehearsal Workflows

Step-by-step guides for band rehearsal capture, 30-minute retroactive memory rescue, multi-mic drum phase alignment, overdub comping, and band mix sharing.

This chapter walks through five end-to-end studio workflows that cover the day-to-day operation of Cave Recorder: capturing a full-band rehearsal with instant stereo reference mixes, rescuing an unrecorded jam from the 30-minute retroactive memory, phase-aligning a multi-microphone drum kit and guitar rig, tracking and comping layered overdubs in Pass Lanes, and mixing with non-destructive Snapshots and Google Drive synchronization.

3.1 Workflow 1: Full-Band Rehearsal Capture & Instant Reference Prints#

Studio Scenario: A four- or five-piece band gathers for a two-hour evening rehearsal. You want to record every song run-through across 12 to 18 channels without slowing down the room, mark strong sections on the fly, and hand every band member compact 24-bit .flac stereo mixes before they finish packing up their gear.

3.1.1 Rehearsal Capture Procedure#

  1. Launch the Session from Your Rehearsal Template:
    • Press CMD + O (macOS) or CTRL + O (Windows/Linux) to open the PROJECT modal.
    • Select your band's Project pill, click Template: 18ch Live Band (or your custom room template), type a Session Name (Tuesday Night Rehearsal) and Session Tag (Rehearsal), and click New Session.
  2. Arm All Active Input Channels:
    • Press CMD + 1 (macOS) or CTRL + 1 (Windows/Linux) to view MIX CONSOLE.
    • Confirm that CH01 through CH18 (or all active mic and DI channels) have their red Record Arm (R) buttons engaged (click R on each strip, or select a strip and press SHIFT + A).
    • Verify that Overdub Mode (OD) in the Top Transport Deck is off (unlit) so each recording starts a fresh t = 0 Take.
  3. Dial a Quick Monitor and Live Print Balance:
    • While the band warms up, verify input levels average around -18 dBFS RMS with peaks around -6 dBFS.
    • Set channel faders and stereo pans (OverheadL/OverheadR, GuitarL/GuitarR, KeysL/KeysR), blend in AUX 1 REVERB (RVB) on snare and vocals, and engage TAPE saturation, the brickwall LIM (CEIL -0.3dB), and the 2-TRK live Print button on the MST MASTER BUS strip. Because the live stereo Print records directly from the Master Bus output whenever 2-TRK is armed, this monitor balance becomes your immediate reference mix.
  4. Start Recording Song 1 (Take 001):
    • Just before the drummer counts in, press R (or click Record ● in the Top Deck).
    • Cave Recorder creates Take 001 at 00:00:00.000 and simultaneously writes raw 32-bit float pre-fader stems (Rec001) into Take001/rec/ and the live stereo Master Bus mix (Print001.wav) into Take001/mix/.
  5. Drop Cue Markers on the Fly (M):
    • Whenever a chorus groove locks in, a bridge arrangement changes, or a solo stands out during the performance, press M on the keyboard.
    • Cave Recorder drops a timestamped standalone marker (M005, M006, ...) at the exact live playhead sample without interrupting recording.
  6. Stop at the End of the Song (SPACE):
    • When the final chord rings out, press SPACE (or SHIFT + SPACE).
    • Cave Recorder closes all open audio files for Take 001 and automatically captures a post-recording console Snapshot (Auto-Save (Post-Recording)).
  7. Capture Subsequent Songs as Independent Takes (Take 002, Take 003, ...):
    • When the band counts in the next run-through or the next song, simply press R again.
    • Cave Recorder automatically increments to Take 002 with a brand-new 00:00:00.000 timeline origin. Repeat R, play the song, and press SPACE for every song of the night.
  8. Tag Keeper Takes and Convert Live Prints to .flac (FLAC PRINT / FLAC ALL PRINTS):
    • At the end of rehearsal, press CMD + 4 (macOS) or CTRL + 4 (Windows/Linux) to open the SESSION REEL.
    • In the TAKES CATALOG, click EDIT TAKE 001 to assign a descriptive song tag (such as NewSong_Run1 or ChorusKeeper).
    • Click FLAC PRINT 001 on individual Takes, or click FLAC ALL PRINTS in the Session Reel header bar to batch-encode every live stereo .wav Print to 24-bit .flac and link it into <ProjectTag>/_Mixes/. (If you adjusted faders after recording and want an updated offline render instead of the live Print, click FLAC on the MST MASTER BUS strip to render an offline Mix001.flac Mixdown).
  9. Sync Reference Mixes to Bandmates' Phones:
    • Click UPLOAD SES MIXES in the Session Reel header (or open the Top Deck CLOUD popover and click SYNC SESSION MIXES).
    • Cave Recorder uploads the compact .flac reference mixes to Google Drive in seconds so every band member can listen on the drive home.
Automatic FLAC Encoding on Cloud Upload

Leave the green AUTO-FLAC ON UPLOAD toggle enabled in the SESSION REEL (CMD + 4 / CTRL + 4) or CLOUD popover. Whenever you click UPLOAD SES MIXES or UPLOAD SES FULL, Cave Recorder automatically converts any uncompressed .wav Prints into 24-bit .flac and populates <ProjectTag>/_Mixes/ prior to uploading.

3.2 Workflow 2: Rescuing an Unrecorded Jam with 30-Minute Retroactive Capture (RETRO)#

Studio Scenario: Between rehearsed songs, the transport is stopped while the guitarist tunes. The drummer and bassist start an improvised groove, the rest of the band joins in, and eight minutes later they finish the best song idea of the night. Nobody pressed Record.

Because Cave Recorder continuously maintains a rolling 30-minute retroactive capture memory across all armed inputs whenever RAM is active, the entire multi-channel performance is still in system memory waiting to be saved.

3.2.1 Retroactive Rescue Procedure#

  1. Keep RAM Active and Channels Armed During Rehearsal:
    • At the start of your session, verify that the RAM button in the Top Transport Deck is illuminated green and all live studio channels (CH01..CH18) have their red R (Record Arm) buttons engaged.
    • Even while the transport is stopped (■), Cave Recorder continuously retains the most recent 30 minutes of incoming 32-bit float audio from every armed channel in memory.
  2. Trigger Retroactive Capture (RETRO):
    • As soon as the unrecorded jam finishes (or even while the band is still playing), click the RETRO (0) button in the Top Transport Deck, or press SHIFT + R.
  3. Verify the Rescued Take:
    • Cave Recorder immediately flushes the rolling multi-channel capture memory to disk as a new numbered Take (Take<NNN>), increments the Top Deck counter badge to RETRO (1), builds the multi-tier waveform overview, and switches the active console and timeline to the rescued Take.
  4. Trim Pre-Jam Room Chatter into a Clean Song Take (SPLIT TAKE):
    • Press CMD + 2 (macOS) or CTRL + 2 (Windows/Linux) to switch to WAVEFORM TIMELINE.
    • Scrub or zoom (ZOOM IN (+) or =, or vertical/horizontal drag on the timeline ruler) to find the exact moment the drummer counted in the jam, and click to place the playhead just before the first beat.
    • Click SPLIT TAKE in the Waveform Timeline toolbar (or press M to drop a marker, open MARKERS & RANGES with CMD + 5 / CTRL + 5, and click NEW TAKE -> END on that marker row).
    • Cave Recorder extracts the jam from that marker forward into its own clean 00:00:00.000 Take and lets you move the earlier tuning Take to the session trash via TOMB TAKE 001 (or permanently remove an unwanted retroactive Take via PURGE RETRO 001) in SESSION REEL (CMD + 4 / CTRL + 4).

3.3 Workflow 3: Multi-Mic Drum & Guitar Cab Phase Alignment (ALIGN ALL & Φ)#

Studio Scenario: You are tracking a full band across 18 channels (CH01 01KickIn, CH02 02SnrTp, CH03 03OvrhdL, CH04 04GtrAmp, CH05 TomRack, CH06 TomFloor, CH07 OverheadL, CH08 OverheadR, CH09 SpdifSynthL, CH10 SpdifSynthR, CH11 BassDi, CH12 BassAmp, CH13 GuitarL, CH14 GuitarR, through CH18 RoomAmbience). Because acoustic wavefronts from the kick and snare travel several feet through the air before reaching the overhead and room microphones, and because a bass amp microphone lags behind a direct Bass DI box, summing the raw channels causes phase cancellation that thins out low-end punch and smears transient focus.

3.3.1 Multi-Mic Phase Alignment Procedure#

  1. Record a 10-to-15 Second Full-Kit & Band Reference Pass:
    • Arm all drum and multi-miked instrument channels (R) in MIX CONSOLE (CMD + 1 / CTRL + 1).
    • Press R and have the drummer play a 10-to-15 second groove that hits the kick, snare, toms, hi-hat, and cymbals clearly while the bassist and guitarist play locked eighth notes.
    • Press SPACE to stop recording.
  2. Verify the Timing Reference Channel (0us):
    • Look at the phase delay readout directly beneath each channel strip's top identifier badge (CH01 01KickIn shows -2.4dB 0us).
    • By default, CH01 (01KickIn or Overhead L) serves as the 0us acoustic timing anchor against which correlated channels are measured.
  3. Run Automatic Multi-Mic Phase Alignment (ALIGN ALL):
    • Click the green ALIGN ALL button in the Top Transport Deck (or Φ ALIGN ALL in the session toolbar bar).
    • To align only a single channel strip (such as aligning CH12 BassAmp to CH11 BassDi), select that strip and click its individual Φ button. Both Trigger Global Multi-Mic Phase Align and Trigger Phase Align on Inspected Track can also be bound to custom keyboard shortcuts in KEYMAP (CMD + 6 / CTRL + 6).
    • To manually flip 180-degree polarity (Ø) on the currently inspected channel strip at any time, click Ø or press SHIFT + P while in MIX CONSOLE.
  4. Inspect Sub-Sample Delay (us) and Polarity (Ø) Corrections:
    • Cave Recorder cross-correlates the recorded multi-channel waveforms and engages the green Φ phase button and blue Ø polarity button where needed:
    • On CH02 02SnrTp, notice the readout updates to +1.5dB 420us with both polarity inversion (Ø blue) and phase delay (Φ green) engaged to lock the snare transient in phase with the kick and overheads.
    • On CH03 03OvrhdL, notice the readout updates to +0.0dB 1850us with Φ engaged to compensate for the acoustic propagation distance between the close mics and overheads.
  5. A/B Audition the Phase Alignment (PHASE: ALIGN vs. PHASE: BYP):
    • Press SPACE to play back the drum recording through your studio monitors.
    • Click PHASE: ALIGN in the Top Deck (or PHASE A/B in the session sub-header) to toggle between PHASE: ALIGN (active) and PHASE: BYP (bypassed).
    • Listen to the low-frequency weight of the kick drum, the body of the snare, and the phantom center image of the kit snap into focus when PHASE: ALIGN is engaged. All subsequent Takes in the Session inherit these aligned delay and polarity settings automatically.

3.4 Workflow 4: Layered Guitar & Vocal Overdubs with Pass Lane Comping (OD)#

Studio Scenario: The band has tracked a solid rhythm section performance in Take 001. Now the guitarist or lead vocalist wants to loop the chorus section, record three alternate solo or vocal passes (Rec002-OD, Rec003-OD, Rec004-OD) inside Take 001, and select the best performance using Pass Lanes.

3.4.1 Overdub Tracking & Pass Lane Comping Procedure#

  1. Select the Target Take (TAKE 001):
    • Use < TAKE 001 > in the Top Deck (ALT + [ / ALT + ]) or click LOAD TAKE 001 in SESSION REEL (CMD + 4 / CTRL + 4) so the rhythm Take is loaded at 00:00:00.000.
  2. Disarm Rhythm Tracks and Arm Only the Overdub Channel:
    • In MIX CONSOLE (CMD + 1 / CTRL + 1), click the red R buttons on the drum and bass strips (CH01..CH03, CH05..CH12) to disarm them so they play back their PASS 01 (Rec001) stems from disk.
    • On the target overdub channel (for example, CH04 04GtrAmp or CH17 LeadVocal), engage both R (Record Arm, or press SHIFT + A while the strip is selected in MIX CONSOLE) and I (Input Monitor, or press SHIFT + I) so the performer hears their live input blended with playback.
  3. Latch Overdub Mode (OD) and Low-Latency Monitoring:
    • Click OD in the Top Transport Deck (or press O) so the OD button illuminates bright red.
    • Click Low-Latency Monitoring in Row 2 of the Top Deck so any high-latency plugins on non-armed buses are bypassed in the live monitoring path.
  4. Define the Section Loop Range (K, L & ⇄):
    • Press CMD + 2 (macOS) or CTRL + 2 (Windows/Linux) to open WAVEFORM TIMELINE.
    • Place the playhead two bars before the solo or chorus entrance and press K (or click RANGE START (K)).
    • Place the playhead a bar after the section ends and press L (or click RANGE END (L)).
    • Press C (or click Cycle/Loop ⇄ in the Top Deck) to enable loop playback across the range.
  5. Record Multiple Overdub Passes (OD 02, OD 03, OD 04):
    • Press R to start overdub recording inside Take 001. While the rhythm tracks play back, Cave Recorder captures the new performance onto CH04 as Rec002-OD (displayed as OD 02) and automatically applies your calibrated roundtrip loopback offset (-640 smp (-13.3ms)).
    • Press SPACE at the end of the pass, return to the start of the range, and press R again to capture OD 03 (Rec003-OD) and OD 04 (Rec004-OD).
  6. Expand Pass Lanes in the Waveform Timeline (E):
    • In WAVEFORM TIMELINE (CMD + 2 / CTRL + 2), press E (or click EXPAND ALL PASSES in the timeline toolbar, or click the red pass-count pill v (2) on an individual track header) to expand the track's Pass Lanes. Press SHIFT + E (or click COLLAPSE ALL PASSES) at any time to fold them back into a single compact track row.
    • The top slice of the expanded track displays PASS 01 (BASE PASS), followed by stacked slices for each recorded Overdub Pass (OD 02, OD 03, OD 04, each badged OVERDUB with its sample span Span: 0..48000 smp and net record-offset -640 smp (-13.3ms)). Notice that the Mix Console channel strip badge also updates to show OD 02 in amber when an Overdub Pass is active.
  7. Audition and Select Active Pass Lanes (Use PASS 01 / ★ OD 02):
    • The currently selected pass on a track is badged ACTIVE COMP LANE and displays a green star button (★ PASS 01 or ★ OD 02), while every other pass is badged STANDBY COMP LANE with a clickable Use PASS 01 or Use OD 02 button.
    • While looping playback (SPACE), click Use PASS 01, Use OD 02, or Use OD 03 on any Pass Lane row to promote that pass immediately as the active playback lane for the track.
    • To silence a pass temporarily without changing selection, click M (Mute) on that Pass Lane row, or click Del to remove a flawed pass from the Take.

3.5 Workflow 5: Mixing, Non-Destructive Snapshots & Band Cloud Sharing#

Studio Scenario: Tracking and overdub selection are complete. Now you want to shape a polished mix using Subgroup Buses and Aux Sends, save several mix variations as Snapshots, compare a vocal-up mix against an earlier Snapshot using Present Stash, render an offline Mixdown and Stem Bounces, and synchronize the project to Google Drive.

3.5.1 Bus Mixing, Snapshot A/B & Cloud Sync Procedure#

  1. Organize Channels into Subgroup Buses (DRUM BUS & INST BUS):
    • In MIX CONSOLE (CMD + 1 / CTRL + 1), click the output routing selector (OUT: SUB 1 v) on all drum channels (CH01..CH08, CH18) to route them to SUB 1 (DRUM BUS).
    • Route synths, bass, guitars, and keys (CH09..CH16) to OUT: SUB 2 v (INST BUS), and route lead vocals (CH17) directly to OUT: MST v (MASTER BUS).
  2. Apply Bus Processing and Spatial Aux Sends (RVB & DLY):
    • Click SEL on the DRUM BUS strip to load its 8-slot processor rack into the Upper Inspector Wells.
    • In the 12-Band Parametric EQ well (2:12B EQ), sculpt a low-shelf boost around 60 Hz and a gentle presence lift at 4.5 kHz while watching the pre/post Dual-Trace RTA spectrum.
    • In the VCA Compressor well (3:VCA CMP), select a 4:1 ratio, engage the sidechain high-pass filter (SC HPF), and set MIX 50% for punchy New York-style parallel drum compression.
    • Use each channel's RVB and DLY send sliders (toggling PST for post-fader send operation) to feed AUX 1 REVERB (EMT 140 Plate zero-latency convolution reverb) and AUX 2 TAPE DLY (CAPSTAN TAPE echo with WOW 18%, DMP 4.2k, and PP ping-pong stereo widening).
  3. Save Named Mix Variations as Manual Snapshots (SNAP MANUAL):
    • Press CMD + S on macOS (CTRL + S on Windows and Linux) from any workspace view, or press CMD + 4 / CTRL + 4 to open SESSION REEL and click SNAP MANUAL in the HISTORY toolbar, to capture your baseline mix as Snap 004 [MANUAL].
    • Return to MIX CONSOLE (CMD + 1 / CTRL + 1) and try a more aggressive mix variation (for example, pushing DRIVE +4.5dB on the Master Tape saturator and riding the lead guitar +2.0 dB).
  4. A/B Compare Historical Snapshots with Present Stash (A/B PRESENT STASH):
    • In SESSION REEL (CMD + 4 / CTRL + 4), scroll to the SNAPSHOTS & PRESENT STASH table and click AUDITION SNAP 001 (or AUDITION SNAP 004).
    • Cave Recorder automatically backs up your live, unsaved console adjustments into the Present Stash and displays the AUDITION MODE: SNAP 001 control bar.
    • While playback is rolling, click A/B PRESENT STASH repeatedly to toggle instantaneously between the historical Snapshot and your live working mix with zero audio dropouts.
    • Click RETURN TO PRESENT (or press BACKSPACE) to restore your live working mix, or click COMMIT SNAPSHOT if you prefer the historical Snapshot and want to adopt it as the new working session state.
  5. Render Offline Mixdowns and Stem Bounces:
    • When your mix is finished, click FLAC on the MST MASTER BUS strip in MIX CONSOLE (CMD + 1 / CTRL + 1) to render an offline stereo Mixdown (Mix001.flac / .wav) linked directly into <ProjectTag>/_Mixes/, or trigger offline per-track Stem Bounces (Bounce001.wav) for archival delivery.
  6. Synchronize to Google Drive (UPLOAD SES MIXES / UPLOAD SES FULL):
    • Click UPLOAD SES MIXES in SESSION REEL (CMD + 4 / CTRL + 4) to push all stereo .flac Prints and Mixdowns to Google Drive for rapid band review.
    • Click UPLOAD SES FULL to synchronize the entire session folder (including 32-bit float rec/ stems, snapshots/, and session history) so remote bandmates who have linked the project can open the full multitrack session on their own studio workstations.

4.1.1 Dual-Path Capture: Raw Pre-Insert Stems Plus Live Processed Prints#

In traditional rehearsal spaces and project studios, engineers face a dilemma when tracking a full band:

  1. The Safe But Uninspiring Approach: Record flat tracks with zero processing so nothing can be ruined by an overly aggressive compressor or EQ setting, leaving the band with dry, unmixed rough bounces at the end of the night.
  2. The Inspiring But Risky Approach: Print heavy compression, EQ, and tape saturation directly onto the multitrack stems, risking permanent distortion or pumping if a guitarist boosts a pedal mid-song.

Cave Recorder resolves this dilemma through a split capture topology:

  • Raw Pre-Fader Multitrack Stems (<TakeFolder>/rec/): Every armed Input Track (R) writes its 32-bit floating-point WAV file (Rec001 Base Pass or Rec002-OD Overdub Pass) immediately after Input Trim (TRM), Polarity (Ø), and Phase Alignment (Φ), before the 8-slot insert rack and channel fader. You can dial in aggressive 8:1 drum compression, heavy tape drive, high-pass filters, or experimental CLAP plugins on the console during rehearsal knowing the underlying multitrack stem on disk remains 100% clean and unclipped.
  • Concurrent Live Stereo Master Print (<TakeFolder>/mix/): Simultaneously, while the transport is recording a Take, the Master Bus (2-TRK enabled) captures the complete post-fader, post-subgroup, post-reverb, and post-limiter stereo mix in real time to a Print<NNN> WAV file. Clicking FLAC on the Master Bus renders a 24-bit .flac mix file and links it into the Project's _Mixes/ folder for immediate band sharing and cloud sync, while you retain every raw multitrack stem for later remixing or offline stem bouncing (covered in Chapter 7: Session Reel, Mixdowns, Snapshots, Disk Layout & Cloud Sync).
Input Trim (TRM), Polarity (Ø), and Phase (Φ) Feed the Stem Recorder

Because TRM, Ø, and Φ sit upstream of the multitrack stem tap, calibrating preamp gain mismatches and aligning multi-mic drum phase at the top of the channel strip ensures your recorded stems are already phase-coherent and properly gain-staged on disk. All downstream insert slots (TAPE, 12B EQ, VCA CMP, CLAP), Aux Sends, Faders, and Subgroup processors remain non-destructive during playback and remixing.

4.2.2 Zone 1 & Zone 2: Track Header, Input Routing, Polarity, Phase & Trim#

At the top of each Input Track strip (CH01 through CH18), the Track Header displays the channel badge (CH01 highlighted in emerald when selected) and the custom scribble-strip name (01KickIn, 02SnrTp, 03OvrhdL, 04GtrAmp). A crimson outline borders the entire selected channel strip to show at a glance which track is currently loaded into the four Upper Console Wells (on the Mix Console view, CMD/CTRL + 1) and the full-screen CHANNEL INSPECTOR view (CMD/CTRL + 3). Immediately beneath the track name, a compact dual telemetry line reports the current fader gain and the sub-sample phase delay offset in microseconds (such as -2.4dB 0us on CH01, +1.5dB 420us on CH02, and +0.0dB 1850us on CH03).

Clicking the Physical Input Selector (IN 01 v) opens the CH NN PHYSICAL INPUT popover menu, allowing you to bind the channel strip to any physical hardware input (IN 01 through IN 18) on your connected USB, Thunderbolt, or ADAT audio interface, or select NONE (PLAYBACK) for dedicated mixdown or virtual instrument return tracks.

Directly to the right of the input dropdown sit two phase-conditioning switches:

  • Ø (Polarity Invert, SHIFT + P on the inspected strip): Reverses the positive and negative waveform excursions by 180°. It illuminates cyan when active (for example, on CH02 02SnrTp when phase-aligning a snare microphone against overheads or a bottom snare mic).
  • Φ (Sub-Sample Phase Alignment): Illuminates emerald when automatic or manual multi-mic time alignment is active on the channel, applying a positive delay between 0.00 ms and +50.00 ms (0 us to 50,000 us) as shown in the header telemetry line. See Chapter 5: Built-In Processors, Phase Alignment & CLAP Plugins for the complete multi-mic alignment workflow.

Below the input and phase row, the horizontal Input Trim (TRM) bar adjusts pre-insert gain from -24.0 dB to +24.0 dB around a center 0.0 dB detent. Use TRM to normalize hot or quiet microphone preamplifiers so that raw stems hit disk around -18 dBFS to -12 dBFS RMS with generous headroom before entering the 8-slot processing rack.

4.2.3 Zone 3 & Zone 4: Insert Rack Summary, Active Pass Badge & Aux Sends#

Zone 3 provides instant status feedback for both insert processing and track-level Pass selection:

  • N OF 8 SLOTS: Reports how many of the channel's eight reorderable processor slots are currently populated (for example, 3 OF 8 SLOTS when 12B EQ, VCA CMP, and AUX SND are active).
  • Active Pass Badge (PASS 01 / OD 02): Displays in gold which track-level Pass is currently active inside the loaded Session Take. PASS 01 indicates the original Rec001 Base Pass captured when the Take began at 00:00:00.000, while OD 02, OD 03, and higher indicate subsequent track-level Overdub Passes (Rec002-OD, Rec003-OD) recorded inside that same Take.
Takes vs. Passes on the Channel Strip

Never confuse a session-level Take (TAKE 001 in the Top Deck transport bar, representing an entire song performance starting at 00:00:00.000) with a track-level Pass (PASS 01 or OD 02 on the channel strip, representing an individual recording pass on that single track within the active Take). Expanding and comping Pass Lanes is covered in Chapter 6: Waveform Timeline, Pass Lanes, Markers & Ranges.

Zone 4 houses the two stereo Auxiliary Sends:

  • RVB (Aux 1 Reverb Send): Feeds the console's stereo convolution reverb processor (AUX 1 REVERB), adjustable from -inf (-60.0 dB) to +12.0 dB by dragging horizontally across the crimson send bar.
  • DLY (Aux 2 Tape Delay Send): Feeds the console's stereo capstan tape echo processor (AUX 2 TAPE DLY), adjustable from -inf (-60.0 dB) to +12.0 dB.
  • 3-Way Send Tap Selector (PRE / EQ / PST): Clicking the tap button to the right of either send cycles through three pickoff points in the channel signal path:
    • PRE (Pre-Insert / Pre-Fader): Taps the signal immediately after TRM, Ø, and Φ, unaffected by channel EQ, compression, fader moves, or channel Mute (M). Ideal for feeding a dedicated musician headphone cue mix.
    • EQ (Post-EQ / Pre-Fader): Taps the signal immediately after the 12B EQ slot (so low-cut filters and resonant notch cuts clean up the send) but before the VCA compressor and channel fader.
    • PST (Post-Fader, Default): Taps the signal after the channel insert rack, fader, and Mute switch so that riding the channel fader up or down naturally scales its wet reverb and delay contribution in proportion.

4.2.4 Zone 5, Zone 6 & Zone 7: Output Routing, Switch Bank, Fader & 40-Segment Meter#

Zone 5 controls where the channel's post-fader stereo image is summed:

  • Output Bus Selector (OUT: SUB 1 v, OUT: SUB 2 v, OUT: MST v): Assigns the channel to a stereo Subgroup Bus (illuminated in crimson when routed to a Subgroup such as OUT: SUB 1 v for DRUM BUS) or directly to the stereo Master Bus (OUT: MST v). In the expanded CHANNEL INSPECTOR view (CMD/CTRL + 3), clicking the Output Bus selector opens the multi-bus routing popover, where a single track or subgroup can route to multiple Subgroup Buses and the Master Bus simultaneously for parallel bus processing.
  • Stereo Pan Bar (PAN): Positions the signal between left (L 100%), center (C), and right (R 100%) using a studio -3.0 dB constant-power panning law so a signal panned hard left or right maintains the same perceived loudness as when centered.

Zone 6 provides the four primary console switches (M, S, R, I). All four switches can be clicked directly on any strip or toggled via SHIFT shortcuts on the currently inspected channel (leaving unmodified single keys free for global transport and marker actions):

  1. M (Mute, SHIFT + M on inspected strip): Silences the post-fader output and PST aux sends. Unmodified M drops a cue marker on the timeline without muting audio, and muting a channel on the Mix Console never interrupts pre-fader multitrack stem recording on an armed track.
  2. S (Solo, SHIFT + S on inspected strip): Engages Solo-in-Place on the monitor bus so you can inspect an individual microphone or subgroup in stereo context while keeping multitrack recording untouched.
  3. R (Record Arm, illuminates red, SHIFT + A on inspected track): Arms the track for multitrack WAV capture when transport Record (R) is engaged, and includes the channel in the rolling 30-minute retroactive memory capture (RAM / RETRO, triggered via SHIFT + R).
  4. I (Input Monitor, SHIFT + I on inspected track): Switches the channel strip's input source between automatic disk playback of the active Pass Lane (PASS 01 / OD 02) and live monitoring of the physical hardware input (IN 01..IN 18). Enable I when rehearsing parts or checking microphone placement while playing back an existing Take.

Zone 7 contains the Logarithmic Studio Fader and 40-Segment Peak/RMS Meter:

  • Fader Cap, Scale & Direct Numeric Entry: Provides smooth logarithmic resolution around the 0.0 dB unity gain mark, ranging from +12.0 dB down to -inf (-60.0 dB), with a dedicated numeric dB readout at the bottom of the meter well (-2.4 dB, +1.5 dB, +0.0 dB). Clicking the numeric dB readout below any fader opens an inline text entry box where you can type an exact decibel value (such as -3.5 or 0) and press ENTER.
  • 40-Segment LED Bargraph: Combines instantaneous digital sample peak detection (including a floating yellow peak-hold segment and red 0.0 dBFS overload latch) with continuous RMS loudness ballistics:
    • Emerald Segments (-48 dB to -12 dB): Nominal tracking and mixing operating zone.
    • Amber Segments (-12 dB to -3 dB): Healthy transient peak zone.
    • Red Cap (-3 dB to 0.0 dBFS): Digital full-scale peak warning. Click CLR CLIP on the Master Bus or in the Channel Inspector (or press X in the Channel Inspector) to clear latched clip indicators.

4.3 Subgroup Buses, Aux Returns & Master Bus#

To the right of the Input Track strips sit the console's stereo summing and return strips: Subgroup Buses (DRUM BUS, INST BUS, expandable up to 16 stereo subgroups), two dedicated Stereo Aux Returns (AUX 1 REVERB and AUX 2 TAPE DLY), and the Master Bus (MST MASTER BUS).

4.3.1 Stereo Subgroup Buses (DRUM BUS / SUB 1, INST BUS / SUB 2 .. SUB 16)#

Subgroup Buses let you sum related Input Tracks (such as all drum microphones into DRUM BUS, or guitars and keys into INST BUS) so you can process the entire group through a shared 8-slot insert rack and control its overall level with a single stereo fader.

Each Subgroup Bus strip provides:

  • Crimson Bus Header & Stereo Readout: Displays the custom bus name (DRUM BUS, INST BUS) and current stereo fader gain (+0.0dB STEREO).
  • SEL (Select Bus for Inspection): Clicking SEL loads the Subgroup Bus into the four Upper Console Wells (CMD/CTRL + 1) and the CHANNEL INSPECTOR view (CMD/CTRL + 3). Every Cave Recorder Subgroup Bus owns a full 8-Slot Reorderable SUB RACK with its own Tape Saturation stage, 12-Band Parametric EQ with Dual-Trace RTA, Analog VCA Bus Compressor, and CLAP plugin slots.
  • LINK (Stereo Dynamics & Gain Link): Illuminates crimson when enabled, locking left and right detector sidechains and gain stages across the stereo bus so off-center floor tom or guitar hits never skew the stereo center image.
  • Subgroup Trim (TRM, -24.0 dB to +24.0 dB): Gain-stages the summed multi-channel signal before it enters the Subgroup's 8-slot SUB RACK. If summing eight hot drum tracks drives the subgroup bus compressor too hard, pull back TRM on DRUM BUS rather than disturbing your individual drum fader balance.
  • SUB RACK Status (3 OF 8 SLOTS): Indicates how many processors are active in the subgroup's insert rack.
  • Subgroup Aux Sends (RVB & DLY): Sends the entire post-rack subgroup mix to AUX 1 REVERB (default -12.0 dB) or AUX 2 TAPE DLY (default -15.0 dB, range -inf to +12.0 dB) to place a whole drum kit or horn section into the same acoustic room.
  • Output Bus Selector (OUT: MST v) & Stereo Balance (BAL): Routes the subgroup to the MASTER BUS (or cascades into downstream higher-numbered subgroups via the multi-bus routing popover in the Channel Inspector) and adjusts left/right stereo balance (L 100%..C..R 100%).
  • Bus Mute (M), Solo (S), Fader (-inf to +12.0 dB) & 40-Segment Meter: Controls final subgroup level and solo isolation. Soloing a Subgroup Bus automatically solos its contributing post-fader input channels in the monitor path.

5.6 Automatic Multi-Mic Phase & Polarity Alignment (ALIGN ALL & Φ)#

When recording a drum kit, guitar cabinet, acoustic piano, or horn section with multiple microphones, physical distance differences between capsules create acoustic arrival-time offsets. Sound travels through studio air at approximately 343 meters per second (1,125 feet per second), meaning every 34.3 cm (13.5 inches) of extra distance between a close microphone and an overhead or room microphone delays the acoustic wavefront by 1.0 millisecond (1000 microseconds, or 48 samples at 48.0 kHz).

5.6.1 Why Multi-Mic Time Offsets Cause Comb Filtering & Hollow Low End#

When two microphones capture the same instrument at slightly different arrival times and are summed together on DRUM BUS or MST MASTER BUS:

  • Frequencies whose half-wavelength matches the time offset arrive 180° out of phase and cancel each other out, carving deep, hollow notches across the frequency spectrum (known as comb filtering).
  • A snare drum captured by both 02SnrTp (close dynamic mic) and 03OvrhdL (condenser mic 63 cm / 25 inches above the snare) suffers a 1.85 ms (1850 us) arrival delay at the overhead capsule. Without phase alignment, summing the overheads with the close snare mic thins out the 200 Hz snare body and smears the initial stick transient into a double flam.
  • Similarly, a snare bottom microphone pointed upward at the snare wires moves in the opposite mechanical direction from the top snare microphone, requiring a 180° polarity inversion (Ø) in addition to time alignment.

5.6.2 Running ALIGN ALL & Auditioning with PHASE: ALIGN / PHASE A/B#

Cave Recorder eliminates manual waveform sliding and guesswork through its built-in Sub-Sample Multi-Mic Phase & Polarity Alignment engine:

  1. Record a Short Pass or Play Back an Existing Take: Have the drummer play the full kit for a few bars (or load any recorded Take on the timeline).
  2. Click ALIGN ALL in the Top Deck (or Φ ALIGN ALL on the Console Bar): Cave Recorder analyzes the acoustic arrival-time relationship and waveform polarity across the microphones in your session and delays closer microphones forward in time to match the furthest reference microphone.
  3. Inspect Per-Channel Ø and Φ Readouts: Within milliseconds, each aligned channel strip updates its header telemetry and phase buttons:
    • Ø (Cyan Polarity Badge, SHIFT + P on inspected strip): Engages automatically on any channel whose acoustic waveform is inverted 180° relative to the reference microphone (such as CH02 02SnrTp or a bottom snare mic).
    • Φ (Emerald Phase Align Badge) & Microsecond Readout: Engages sub-sample time alignment (0.00 ms to +50.00 ms, displayed in microseconds from 0us to 50000us, such as 420us on CH02 and 1850us on CH03) so all wavefronts arrive at the subgroup summing bus at the exact same sub-sample instant.
  4. A/B Audition Globally (PHASE: ALIGN / PHASE A/B): Click the emerald PHASE: ALIGN button in the Top Deck (or the PHASE A/B button on the Mix Console toolbar) to toggle all phase and polarity corrections in and out across the entire console simultaneously. You will immediately hear the kick and snare low-end punch tighten up and the stereo center image snap into focus. Both Run Multi-Mic Phase Alignment and Toggle Global Phase A/B can also be bound to custom keyboard shortcuts in the KEYMAP view (CMD/CTRL + 6).

5.7.2 Automatic Plugin Delay Compensation (PDC) & Transparency Breakdown#

All built-in Cave Recorder processors (TAPE, 12B EQ, VCA CMP, AUX 1 REVERB, AUX 2 TAPE DLY) operate with zero internal sample latency (0 smp / 0.00 ms). To prevent phase cancellation or timing drift between parallel tracks and subgroups, Cave Recorder performs Automatic Plugin Delay Compensation (PDC) across the entire mixing graph:

  • The Top Deck displays a live PDC: N smp (X.XX ms @ 48 kHz) status pill at all times.
  • Clicking the PDC pill in the Top Deck opens the Latency & PDC Transparency Breakdown popover (shown in Figure 5.4 above), which reports every latency stage in your studio system in both Samples (smp) and Milliseconds (ms):
    • Audio I/O: Current hardware buffer size (256 frames @ 48000 Hz), input buffer latency (In: 64 smp / 1.33 ms), output buffer latency (Out: 64 smp / 1.33 ms), and calibrated hardware Roundtrip I/O (640 smp / 13.33 ms).
    • Total Graph PDC & Effective Playback PDC: Total delay compensation applied across parallel tracks and buses (Max Safe Ceiling: 96,000 smp / 2000.00 ms).
    • Playhead Lag & Overdub Record-Offset: Exact negative sample offset (-640 smp / 13.33 ms) applied automatically when writing new Overdub Passes (Rec002-OD) to disk so that everything a musician plays against playback lands sample-accurately on the timeline.
    • Filterable Per-Channel Table (ALL, ARMED, COMPENSATED, BYPASSED): Lists every Input Track (Track 1 [ARMED]..Track 18), Subgroup 1..2, Aux 1 (Reverb), Aux 2 (Delay), and Master Bus with its individual sample and millisecond compensation state.

5.7.3 Low-Latency Monitoring During Overdubs#

When you are late in a mix and have inserted a high-latency lookahead mastering plugin or linear-phase EQ on a track or Subgroup Bus, recording a last-minute vocal or guitar overdub through that high-latency plugin would introduce a distracting echo in the musician's headphones.

Clicking Low-Latency Monitoring in the Top Deck solves this immediately:

  • Cave Recorder temporarily bypasses high-latency CLAP plugins only on record-armed (R) live monitoring paths and their downstream buses, dropping the musician's headphone monitoring latency back down to pure zero-latency built-in console processing (0 smp graph PDC).
  • All unarmed playback tracks continue playing with full Plugin Delay Compensation.
  • As soon as you finish recording the Overdub Pass and disarm the track (or toggle Low-Latency Monitoring off in the Top Deck), the high-latency CLAP plugins re-engage automatically without losing a single plugin parameter setting.

6.2 Recording Overdubs (OD) & Comping in Expandable Pass Lanes (PASSES)#

In Cave Recorder, session-level songs and track-level overdubs are strictly separated so your timeline never becomes cluttered:

  • Base Pass (Rec001, labeled PASS 01): When Overdub Mode (OD) is off (the default), pressing Record (R) creates a brand-new session Take starting at 00:00:00.000. Every armed channel strip records its initial multitrack stem as Rec001 (PASS 01).
  • Overdub Pass (Rec002-OD, Rec003-OD, ..., labeled OD 02, OD 03, ...): When you want to layer a second guitar solo, punch in a vocal fix, or try three alternate bass lines inside the currently active Take, latch the red OD button in the Top Deck transport bar (O) and arm only the target track(s). Pressing Record (R) plays back the existing tracks of the active Take from 00:00:00.000 while recording a new Overdub Pass (OD 02, OD 03, ...) into the active Take's rec/ folder and appending a new Pass Lane under each armed track.

6.2.1 Step-by-Step: Recording Multi-Pass Overdubs on a Track#

  1. Load the Target Take: Use the < / > buttons beside TAKE 001 in the Top Deck (ALT + [ / ALT + ]) or click LOAD TAKE in the SESSION REEL view (CMD/CTRL + 4) to load the Take you want to overdub onto.
  2. Disarm Backing Tracks & Arm the Overdub Track: On the Mix Console or in the left track header column of the WAVEFORM TIMELINE view, disarm (R off) the backing band tracks (drums, rhythm section) so they stream from disk, and arm (R lit crimson) only the channel(s) you are overdubbing. Enable input monitoring (I / MON) if you are monitoring through Cave Recorder's console EQ and compression.
  3. Latch Overdub Mode (OD): Click OD in the Top Deck transport bar (or press O) so the OD indicator illuminates red.
  4. Optional Pre-Roll or Cycle Range: Position the Playhead a few bars before the section you want to overdub, or enable Cycle Loop (⇄, C) across a defined range (Section 6.3).
  5. Record the Overdub Pass: Press Record (R) to begin playback and capture OD 02. Press Stop (SPACE or SHIFT + SPACE) when finished. Cave Recorder automatically promotes the newly recorded Overdub Pass (★ OD 02) as the active playback Pass on that track and captures a post-recording Snapshot.
Automatic Overdub Latency Compensation

When you record an Overdub Pass (OD 02), Cave Recorder automatically compensates the recorded Pass Lane using your calibrated hardware loopback offset and live monitoring latency (displayed in the Pass Lane header as -640 smp (-13.3ms)). Backing tracks stream from disk with negative read-ahead compensation so your new overdub lands phase-aligned with the original PASS 01 drum and rhythm stems. See Chapter 2: Hardware Setup, Audio Connections & First Session for loopback calibration details.

6.2.2 Expanding Pass Lanes, Auditioning Passes & Comping Phrases#

When a track within the active Take has more than one recorded Pass, the disclosure badge in the track header displays the total Pass count (for example, ▶ (2) on 02 Snare Top and 03 Overhead L).

  • Expand or Collapse a Single Track's Pass Lanes:
    • Click the ▶ (N) / ▼ (N) badge in the track header on the WAVEFORM TIMELINE view.
    • Or select the track and click PASSES in the Upper Mini-Timeline Well (or press E while in the CHANNEL INSPECTOR view).
  • Expand or Collapse All Tracks' Pass Lanes:
    • Click EXPAND ALL PASSES (E) or COLLAPSE ALL PASSES (SHIFT + E) in the upper-right WAVEFORM TIMELINE toolbar.

When expanded, each track unrolls its vertical stack of Pass Lanes in chronological order (BASE PASS PASS 01 at the top, followed by OVERDUB OD 02, OD 03, and so on):

  • Visual Active vs. Standby Distinction: The currently active Pass Lane (labeled ACTIVE COMP LANE with a green ★ PASS 01 or ★ OD 02 badge) renders its waveform in bright amber-copper. Inactive Pass Lanes (labeled STANDBY COMP LANE with a Use PASS 01 or Use OD 02 button) render in muted slate-grey so you can immediately see which Pass is feeding the Mix Console channel strip.
  • Auditioning & Promoting Passes:
    • Promoting a Pass on the WAVEFORM TIMELINE View: Click Use PASS 01 (or Use OD 02, Use OD 03) in any standby Pass Lane header to promote that Pass as the active playback lane (ACTIVE COMP LANE) immediately. Because playback readers pre-buffer ahead of the Playhead, switching Passes during playback crossfades cleanly without clicks.
    • Promoting a Pass from the MIX CONSOLE or CHANNEL INSPECTOR View: Click PASSES in the Upper Mini-Timeline Well (or press E in the CHANNEL INSPECTOR view) to expand the inspected channel's Pass Lanes, and click Use PASS <NN> or Use OD <NN> on the Pass you want to hear.
  • Per-Pass Mute (M) & Soft Delete (Del):
    • Click M on a Pass Lane to silence that Pass without changing your channel strip Mute state.
    • Click Del on a flawed Pass Lane to mark that Pass as deleted in the session history. Because deletion is non-destructive, pressing CMD/CTRL + Z restores the Pass immediately.
  • Comping Phrases Across Multiple Passes:
    • When an overdub was punched in over a specific chorus or solo section (for example, Span: 240000..480000 smp), promoting that Overdub Pass plays your new performance across its recorded span while allowing you to compare it directly against the underlying PASS 01 Base Pass or alternate OD 03 passes. Every Pass promotion is recorded in the Undo history and stored in Console Snapshots (Chapter 7: Session Reel, Mixdowns, Snapshots, Disk Layout & Cloud Sync) so you can save and compare multiple comp combinations across the session.

6.3 Dropping Cue Markers & Defining Cycle Loop Ranges (MARKERS & RANGES)#

During a live rehearsal jam or playback review, you can drop timestamped Markers and define bounded Ranges without stopping the transport. Markers and Ranges appear both as interactive flags in the MKR and RNG lanes of the WAVEFORM TIMELINE view and as sortable, searchable rows in the dedicated MARKERS & RANGES workspace (CMD/CTRL + 5).

6.3.1 Dropping & Navigating Cue Markers on the Fly (M)#

Every Take automatically initializes with two boundary markers (M001 Take Start at 00:00:00.000 and M002 Take End at the end of the longest recorded stem, forming the default R001 Full Take range). To mark song transitions, solo entrances, or mix notes:

  1. Drop a Marker (M): Press M at any time during live recording or playback (or click + MARKER (M) in the WAVEFORM TIMELINE toolbar or ADD MARKER (M) in the MARKERS & RANGES toolbar). Cave Recorder places a cyan standalone marker flag (M005 Marker 05, M006 Marker 06) at the exact sample position of the rolling Playhead (or at the Position Cursor when transport is stopped).
  2. Reposition a Marker by Dragging: In the MKR MARKERS lane of the WAVEFORM TIMELINE view, hover over any cyan marker flag to inspect its metadata, or click and drag the marker handle left or right to fine-tune its sample timestamp.
  3. Jump Between Markers: Use the toolbar buttons or keyboard shortcuts to navigate between cue points instantly:
    • Previous Marker (< PREV / < PREV MKR): Press [.
    • Next Marker (NEXT > / NEXT MKR >): Press ].
    • First Marker in Take (|< FIRST / |< FIRST MKR): Press SHIFT + [ or HOME.
    • Last Marker in Take (LAST >| / LAST MKR >|): Press SHIFT + ] or END.

6.3.2 Defining Ranges (K / L) & Cycle Loop Playback (⇄)#

A Range (R001, R003, ...) is a named time span bounded by a RANGE START marker and a RANGE END marker. Ranges are used for Cycle Loop playback, overdub punch/loop rehearsal, zooming to a song section, and extracting a song section as a new Take.

  1. Mark Range Start (RANGE START (K)): Move the Playhead or Position Cursor to the beginning of the phrase (or press it live as the band hits the downbeat) and press K (or click RANGE START (K) / SET RANGE START (K)). An amber status pill (RNG T01: START @ 00:00:00.853) appears in the toolbar indicating that a range start boundary is pending.
  2. Mark Range End (RANGE END (L)): When playback reaches the end of the section, press L (or click RANGE END (L) / SET RANGE END (L)). Cave Recorder pairs the start and end boundaries, creates a new numbered Range span (such as R003 Range 03) in the amber RNG RANGES lane, and selects it as the active loop range.
  3. Cancel a Pending Boundary (ESC): If you pressed K by mistake, press ESC or click CANCEL PENDING RANGE to discard the uncompleted boundary.
  4. Select, Drag, or Cycle Overlapping Ranges: In the RNG RANGES lane above the waveforms, click any amber range bar to make it the active range (clicking repeatedly on overlapping spans cycles through them). Drag the left [ or right ] handle of any range bar in the RNG lane to adjust its start or end sample directly on the timeline.
  5. Toggle Cycle Loop Playback (⇄): Click the Cycle Loop button ⇄ in the Top Deck transport bar (or press C). When ⇄ is illuminated amber, playback loops continuously between the start and end boundaries of the active Range (or across R001 Full Take if no sub-range is selected).

6.3.3 Managing, Renaming & Filtering in the MARKERS & RANGES View#

Pressing CMD/CTRL + 5 (or clicking MARKERS & RANGES in the workspace navigation bar) opens the MARKERS & RANGES management workspace, divided into two synchronized tables: MARKERS (top) and RANGES (bottom).

  • Session-Wide vs. Per-Take Filtering: Use the top filter bar (ALL TAKES (SESSION), FILTER TAKE 001, FILTER TAKE 002) to view cue points across every Take in the evening's Session at once, or isolate markers belonging to a single Take. Clicking VIEW TAKE 001 TIMELINE jumps directly to WAVEFORM TIMELINE with that Take loaded.
  • Marker Type Filtering & Automatic Color Badges: Filter the upper table by ALL, STANDALONE (crimson STANDALONE badges, such as M005), or BOUNDARY (slate RANGE START and RANGE END badges paired to a Range, such as RANGE START (R003)), or type into Search markers... to filter by marker name or description note.
  • Per-Marker Actions (GO TO, EDIT, -> RNG START, -> RNG END, DEL):
    • GO TO: Loads the owning Take (if not already active) and snaps the transport Playhead directly to that marker's exact sample position.
    • EDIT: Opens inline editing fields to update the marker's Name (for example, Verse 2 Harmonies) and free-form Description note (SAVE M<NNN> / CANCEL), or click PRESET MARKER HIGHLIGHT M<NNN> to emphasize key cue points.
    • -> RNG START / -> RNG END: Promotes an existing standalone cue marker into the start or end boundary of a Range so you can drop single-key M markers during a live jam and turn them into bounded song ranges afterward.
    • DEL: Deletes the marker (reversible via Undo CMD/CTRL + Z).
  • Range Filtering (ALL / FROM STANDALONE) & Per-Range Actions (ZOOM RANGE, NEW TAKE, EDIT, DEL):
    • FROM STANDALONE Filter Pill: Filters the RANGES table (alongside ALL) to show only Ranges whose start or end boundaries were promoted from standalone cue markers via -> RNG START / -> RNG END.
    • ZOOM RANGE: Switches to the WAVEFORM TIMELINE view and zooms the horizontal viewport to frame that exact Range from edge to edge.
    • EDIT: Opens inline fields to rename the Range (for example, R003 Chorus Loop or Song 2 - Midnight Drive) and update its description.

6.4 Non-Destructive Take Splitting (Split Take at Playhead / Split by Markers)#

During a band rehearsal, musicians often hit Record once and play a continuous 25-minute block containing warm-up banter, three separate songs, and an improvised jam. Cave Recorder lets you carve that long continuous capture into independent, self-contained t = 0 Takes (Take 002, Take 003, Take 004) so each song gets its own 00:00:00.000 timeline, its own song tag, and its own stereo mixdown.

6.4.1 Three Ways to Split or Extract Independent Takes#

Cave Recorder provides three non-destructive workflows depending on whether you want to divide a Take in two or extract a specific marker/range span:

Split / Extract Method Where to Trigger How It Works
1. Split Take at Playhead / Cursor (SPLIT TAKE) Click SPLIT TAKE in the WAVEFORM TIMELINE toolbar Divides the active Take at the current Playhead/Cursor timestamp into two consecutive segments: the first part (00:00:00.000 to the split point) remains in the current Take, and the second part (from the split point to the end) becomes a brand-new t = 0 Take (Take<NNN>).
2. Extract from Marker to End (NEW TAKE -> END) Click NEW TAKE -> END on any standalone marker row in the MARKERS & RANGES view (CMD/CTRL + 5) Extracts everything from that marker's sample timestamp through the end of the Take into a brand-new t = 0 Take (Take<NNN>), leaving the source Take's full duration untouched.
3. When you divide a Take with SPLIT TAKE or extract a span with NEW TAKE:

  2. Sample-Zero (t = 0) Alignment: Every multitrack stem in the newly created Take (Take<NNN>) starts at its own 00:00:00.000 timeline zero aligned to the split or range start point, with Broadcast Wave (bext) timecode metadata preserved.
  3. Pass Structure & Console Routing Preserved: All recorded Base Passes (Rec001) and Overdub Passes (Rec002-OD) that overlap the extracted span are preserved in their respective Pass Lanes, along with per-track phase alignment offsets, latency compensation metadata, and internal markers shifted relative to the new 00:00:00.000 origin.
  4. Immediate Session Reel Registration: The newly created Take appears immediately in the SESSION REEL catalog (Chapter 7: Session Reel, Mixdowns, Snapshots, Disk Layout & Cloud Sync) ready for tagging, live Print conversion, or offline Mixdown rendering.

Cave Recorder processes the entire Take from 00:00:00.000 through the full console signal path, increments the sequential mix number (Mix001, Mix002, ...), writes the file into <TakeFolder>/mix/, and links it into <ProjectTag>/_Mixes/.
  3. Render Processed Track or Subgroup Stems (Bounce<NNN>): When exporting stems for an external mix or mastering session, Cave Recorder renders Individual Track Stems (01-Kick-In, 02-Snare-Top, ...) and/or Stereo Subgroup Bus Stems (Bus01-Drum-Bus, Bus02-Guitar-Bus, ...) into <TakeFolder>/bounce/ in WAV or FLAC. 7.3 Non-Destructive Console Snapshots & Present Stash (SNAPS)#

A Console Snapshot (Snap 001, Snap 002, ...) is a complete, point-in-time capture of your entire mixing console and timeline comp state stored as a compact JSON file inside <SessionFolder>/snapshots/. Because Snapshots reference your existing audio stems rather than duplicating .wav files on disk, you can save dozens of mix variations per Session with near-zero disk footprint.

7.3.1 What a Snapshot Captures & Automatic Snapshot Policies#

Every Snapshot records:

  • All input channel strip parameters (input trim, 180-degree polarity invert, microsecond acoustic phase delay, reorderable 8-slot processing rack order, 12-band Parametric EQ nodes, VCA Compressor settings, Tape Saturation, CLAP plugin states, Aux send levels and tap points, pan, fader gain, mute, solo, and output bus routing).
  • All stereo Subgroup Bus (SUB 1..16), Aux Return (AUX 1 Convolution Reverb, AUX 2 Stereo Tape Delay), and Master Bus parameters.
  • Active Pass Lane selections (PASS 01, OD 02, ...) and per-pass mute states across every Take in the Session.
  • The complete compact Undo/Redo history stack at that moment in time, so restoring a Snapshot also restores its undo timeline.

Snapshots are created in three ways and can be filtered in the SNAPSHOTS & PRESENT STASH table at the bottom of the SESSION REEL view using the ALL, MANUAL, and AUTO pills:

  1. Manual Snapshot ([MANUAL]): Click SNAP MANUAL in the Session Reel history bar or press CMD/CTRL + S from any view to save a named milestone (for example, Manual Snapshot 04 or Pre-Vocal-Overdub Balance).
  2. Post-Recording Auto-Save ([POST-REC]): Automatically captured immediately after every normal recording stop (Auto-Save (Post-Recording)), preserving the exact monitor mix and Pass state that was active when the band finished the Take. (If you abort a false start using the clean-restart discard stop command, the post-recording auto-save is suppressed.)
  3. Periodic 20-Minute Auto-Save ([AUTO]): Automatically captured every 20 minutes while a Session is open, provided at least one console or timeline action has occurred since the last saved Snapshot.

7.3.2 Auditioning Historical Snapshots with Present Stash Protection#

In traditional mixing consoles, loading an old scene or snapshot overwrites your current unsaved fader moves unless you remember to save a temporary file first. Cave Recorder eliminates this hazard with Present Stash:

  1. Click AUDITION SNAP <NNN> to Enter Audition Mode: The moment you click AUDITION SNAP 001 on any row in the SNAPSHOTS & PRESENT STASH table while working on a live mix, Cave Recorder automatically saves your current unsaved working console state into Present Stash in memory and illuminates the burgundy AUDITION MODE: SNAP 001 | VIEWING: HISTORICAL SNAPSHOT banner (see Figure 7.2).
  2. Click Through Multiple Historical Snapshots Freely: While in Audition Mode, you can click AUDITION SNAP 002, AUDITION SNAP 003, AUDITION SNAP 004, and so on across five or more historical Snapshots to compare balances. Your original working mix remains safely locked inside Present Stash the entire time.
  3. Instant Zero-Latency Comparison (A/B PRESENT STASH): Click A/B PRESENT STASH in the Audition Mode banner to toggle directly between the historical Snapshot and your stashed present mix while audio is playing. All fader, EQ, and dynamics changes transition via sample-accurate smoothing ramps (5 to 10 ms) without audio clicks.
  4. Restore Your Working Mix (RETURN TO PRESENT or BACKSPACE): Click RETURN TO PRESENT in the Audition Mode banner (or press BACKSPACE while in the SESSION REEL view) to exit Audition Mode and restore your exact working console state from Present Stash with zero lost fader or EQ moves.
  5. Adopt the Historical Snapshot (COMMIT SNAPSHOT): If you decide you prefer the historical Snapshot over your previous working state, click COMMIT SNAPSHOT to promote that Snapshot as your active live session mix.

You never need to manually save a throwaway snapshot before checking how the mix sounded an hour ago. Present Stash engages automatically on the first AUDITION SNAP click and guards your live mix until you explicitly click RETURN TO PRESENT (BACKSPACE) or COMMIT SNAPSHOT.

Key studio rules governing disk files:

  • 08:00 Local Time Session-Day Rollover: A studio session day runs from 08:00 to 07:59 local time the following morning. If your band starts rehearsing on October 15 at 21:00 and records a Take after midnight at 01:30 on October 16, that late-night recording still belongs to session date 20261015 and receives a four-digit <Stamp> (0130). If you open the same Session four days later on October 19 to record a guitar overdub, <Stamp> automatically expands to include the calendar date (20261019-1922).
  • Permanent File IDs vs. Editable Folder Tags: The <SessionID>, Take numbers (Take001, Take002), Pass numbers (Rec001, Rec002-OD), and timestamps are assigned once at creation and never change, and numbers are never reused after a deletion. Editable tags (<SessionTag> like Jam and <TakeTag> like Improv) appear only in folder names, never inside .wav or .flac filenames. As a result, you can rename song tags and display titles freely at any time without breaking file references or re-uploading gigabytes of audio to the cloud.
  • Broadcast Wave (bext) Headers & Sample-Zero Alignment: Every .wav stem is written with standard Broadcast Wave (bext) metadata embedding the wall-clock origin timecode while aligning the start of the Take to sample 0 (00:00:00.000).

7.5.4 Exclusive Session Write-Locking (Read-Only Preview) & Zero-Loss Conflict Merging#

When multiple bandmates collaborate on the same Session across different computers, Cave Recorder prevents accidental overwrites using Exclusive Session Write-Locking paired with Snapshot-Backed Conflict Merging:

  • Automatic Write Lock on Open & Release on Close:
    • When you open a Session while online, Cave Recorder acquires an exclusive edit lock for your computer and automatically pulls any newer cloud metadata if your local copy has no unsaved edits.
    • When you close the Session (CLOSE SESSION) or quit Cave Recorder while online, the edit lock is released automatically so another bandmate can open the Session for editing.
  • Opening a Session Locked by a Bandmate (Read-Only Preview Mode):
    • If a bandmate currently has the Session open on another computer, Cave Recorder alerts you and offers OPEN READ-ONLY PREVIEW (recommended), BREAK LOCK & TAKE WRITE ACCESS, or CANCEL.
    • In Read-Only Preview mode, an amber READ-ONLY (LOCKED BY <MACHINE>) badge appears in the header. You have full playback, timeline scrubbing, and live in-memory console mixing (faders, solos, 12-band EQ, VCA compression, and Snapshot auditioning), while transport Record / Overdub and background disk writes are disabled so you never conflict with your bandmate's live recording session.
    • If you dial in a great mix while in Read-Only Preview mode, click SAVE AS LOCAL SNAPSHOT in the header bar to preserve your mix settings locally, or click TAKE LOCK once your bandmate finishes.
  • Zero-Loss Merging of Offline Edits, Takes & Deletions:
    • Audio files in rec/, mix/, and bounce/ are never overwritten, and new recordings and bounces are always saved as new numbered files. Pulling a MIXES update onto the studio computer that holds the raw rec/ multitrack WAV stems adds new mixes and snapshots without ever deleting local multitrack stems.
    • Takes or Passes that were marked as deleted in the session history on one machine remain marked as deleted across syncs so purged false starts are never re-downloaded.
    • If two bandmates both edit a Session's mix while offline and later sync to Google Drive, Cave Recorder detects that both computers edited the Session while offline, combines all newly recorded Takes, Passes, Mixdowns, and Snapshots from both machines, and prompts you to choose either KEEP LOCAL MIX & SAVE CLOUD MIX AS SNAPSHOT or ADOPT CLOUD MIX & SAVE LOCAL MIX AS SNAPSHOT. The alternate mix is automatically saved into snapshots/ so zero fader or EQ work is ever lost, and you can immediately compare both bandmates' mixes side by side using Present Stash (Section 7.3.2).

Every row in the KEYMAP table includes an interactive SHORTCUT (CLICK TO ASSIGN) capture well and row action buttons on the far right:

  2. 8.3.3 Live Scope Conflict Detection, Shared Non-Conflicting Scopes & OS Warnings#

When you assign a custom shortcut, Cave Recorder validates the key chord against two safety layers before writing to disk:

  1. Scope Clash Detection (CLASH DETECTED Confirmation Banner):
    • Overlapping Scopes (Blocked Without Confirmation): Because GLOBAL shortcuts are active across every workspace view, assigning the same key chord to two GLOBAL actions, to two actions in the same workspace view, or to a GLOBAL action and a workspace view action creates an ambiguous collision. When you click APPLY on a colliding chord, Cave Recorder pauses and displays the amber Clash Detection Banner at the top of the KEYMAP view (CLASH DETECTED: [SPACE] conflicts with "Play / Pause Transport" (GLOBAL) in overlapping scope [GLOBAL]. Overwrite and unbind existing?). Click CONFIRM OVERWRITE to assign the shortcut and automatically unbind the conflicting action, or click CANCEL to keep your existing bindings untouched.
    • Non-Conflicting Sibling Views (Allowed & Highlighted in Green): Because only one workspace view tab is visible at a time, sibling view scopes (MIX CONSOLE, WAVEFORM TIMELINE, CHANNEL INSPECTOR, SESSION REEL, and MARKERS & RANGES) never conflict with each other, and PROJECT MODAL only runs while the modal browser is open. When two actions in separate views share the same key (such as E bound to Expand All Track Pass Lanes in WAVEFORM TIMELINE and Toggle Pass Lanes on Inspected Channel in CHANNEL INSPECTOR), the DESCRIPTION, SCOPE & WARNINGS column highlights SHARED IN NON-CONFLICTING SCOPE: CHANNEL INSPECTOR in emerald green.
  2. Operating System Shortcut Conflict Warnings (OS WARN):
    • If you assign a key combination commonly intercepted by the host desktop environment, Cave Recorder displays an amber OS WARN (<Platform>): <System Function> badge in the row description so you know the operating system may capture the key press first:
    • macOS Warnings: CMD + Q (Quit Application), CMD + H (Hide Window), CMD + M (Minimize Window), CMD + W (Close Window), CMD + SPACE (Spotlight Search), CMD + TAB (Application Switcher), CMD + SHIFT + 3/4/5 (System Screenshot), and CTRL + Arrow keys (Mission Control / Desktop Spaces).
    • Windows & Linux Warnings: ALT + F4 (Close Window), ALT + TAB (Window Switcher), ALT + SPACE (Window Menu), CTRL + W / CTRL + Q (Close / Quit), CTRL + SHIFT + ESC (Task Manager), CTRL + ALT + T / DELETE (Terminal / Session Dialog), and SUPER (WIN) + L/D/E/R/I/TAB/SPACE/Arrow keys.

9.1 Take vs. Pass Disambiguation (Quick Guide)#

Traditional desktop digital audio workstations use the word "Take" for both an entire song recording and an overdubbed layer on a single track, causing constant confusion during band rehearsals. Cave Recorder enforces a strict four-tier studio hierarchy where Session-level Takes and Track-level Passes are completely distinct concepts:

Project (Band / Artist Workspace, e.g., GF)
 └── Session (Rehearsal Night or Tracking Day, e.g., GF_20261020-1)
      └── Take (Complete Song Run-Through Starting at 00:00:00.000)
           └── Pass (Single-Track Stem Inside That Take: PASS 01 / OD 02)
Studio Term & Level UI Badge & Location On-Disk Pattern How It Is Created & Used
Project (Level 1: Band or venture) PROJECT modal (CMD/CTRL + O) <LibraryRoot>/<ProjectTag>/ Top-level band folder containing all Sessions plus a unified _Mixes/ folder for rapid listening and cloud sharing.
Session (Level 2: Rehearsal or studio day) Session pill in Top Deck & SESSION REEL (CMD/CTRL + 4) <ProjectTag>/<SessionID>_<Tag>/ Defines the room's 18-channel microphone patch, strip names, bus routing, and console processing shared across all Takes recorded that day.
Take (Level 3: Complete song or jam at t = 0) < TAKE 001 > in Top Deck & TAKES CATALOG Take<NNN>_<Stamp>[_<TakeTag>]/ Created whenever you press Record (R) with Overdub Mode (OD) off. Every Take starts at its own 00:00:00.000 origin and is never stacked on top of another Take.
Base Pass (Level 4A: Initial track stem) PASS 01 (BASE PASS) on strip & Pass Lane rec/*_<CC>-<Name>_Rec001_<Stamp>.wav The initial 32-bit float pre-fader mono stem captured on a record-armed channel strip when the Take was first recorded from 00:00:00.000.
Overdub Pass (Level 4B: Layered track stem) OD 02, OD 03 (OVERDUB) on strip & Pass Lane rec/*_<CC>-<Name>_Rec<MMM>-OD_<Stamp>.wav A subsequent track-level recording captured inside the currently active Take when Overdub Mode (OD, O) is latched on the Top Deck.
Pass Lanes (Track sub-lanes inside a Take) PASSES (E / SHIFT + E) in WAVEFORM TIMELINE Stored in *_Take<NNN>_take.json Expandable vertical sub-lanes under a track displaying its PASS 01 Base Pass and OD 02..N Overdub Passes for one-click comp selection (Use PASS 01 / ★ OD 02).
Pass Lanes Are Never Called Take Lanes

In Cave Recorder, track-level overdub lanes under a channel are always called Pass Lanes (PASS 01, OD 02, OD 03) and are never called "Take Lanes", and the project and session browser modal is always labeled PROJECT and is never called a "Welcome Screen" or "Welcome Dialog". A Take (TAKE 001, TAKE 002) is always an entire multitrack song performance starting at 00:00:00.000, while a Pass is a single track's recording layer inside that Take.

9.2 Studio Glossary#

The alphabetical definitions below cover every core studio concept, signal-processing stage, console switch, and workflow term in Cave Recorder, with direct links to the chapters where each topic is covered in depth.

12-Band Parametric EQ (2:12B EQ)#

Zero-latency 12-band precision state-variable parametric equalizer (B1 through B12) available in the 8-slot processor rack of every Input Track (CH01..CH18) and Subgroup Bus (SUB 1..SUB 16). Each band provides -24.0 dB to +24.0 dB gain across 20 Hz to 20 kHz, variable Q (0.10 to 18.00), and seven selectable filter shapes: peaking Bell (BELL), Low Shelf (LSHF), High Shelf (HSHF), High-Pass Filter (HPF), Low-Pass Filter (LPF), surgical Notch (NTCH), and Spectral Tilt (TILT). See Section 5.3.

30-Minute Retroactive Capture (RAM / RETRO)#

Continuous background audio capture system that retains the most recent 30 minutes of live 32-bit floating-point audio across all record-armed (R) input channels in system memory whenever the green RAM button is lit in the Top Transport Deck. Clicking RETRO (or pressing SHIFT + R) flushes the rolling 30-minute memory to disk as a new numbered Session Take (Take<NNN>) so spontaneous jams played while the transport was stopped are never lost. See Section 1.1.2, Section 2.2.3, and Section 3.2.

8-Slot Processor Rack#

Reorderable eight-slot insert processing chain (Slots 1..8) on every Input Track and Subgroup Bus. Holds Cave Recorder's zero-latency built-in processors (2:12B EQ, 3:VCA CMP, 4:AUX SND movable send tap) alongside third-party .clap audio plugins (1:CLAP). Slots can be reordered live during playback by dragging their :: handle or clicking ^ / v with clickless crossfading, or bypassed individually via B. See Section 4.1 and Section 5.1.3.

Active Comp Lane (ACTIVE COMP LANE)#

The single Pass Lane currently selected for disk playback on a track inside the active Take. Highlighted in amber-copper waveform color with a green star badge (★ PASS 01 or ★ OD 02) in WAVEFORM TIMELINE (CMD/CTRL + 2) and reflected on the channel strip's gold pass indicator badge on the Mix Console. See Section 3.4 and Section 6.2.2.

Analog VCA Compressor (3:VCA CMP)#

Zero-latency voltage-controlled amplifier dynamics processor included in every Input Track and Subgroup Bus 8-slot rack. Provides adjustable Threshold (-60.0 dB to 0.0 dB), Ratio (1.0:1 to 20.0:1 with 2:1, 4:1, 8:1, and 20:1 presets), Attack (0.1 ms to 100.0 ms), Release (10.0 ms to 1000.0 ms), Makeup Gain (0.0 dB to +24.0 dB), a 90 Hz Sidechain High-Pass Filter (SC HPF, shortcut H in Channel Inspector), Stereo Link (LINK), New York parallel blend (MIX 50%), and a real-time Gain Reduction (GR) trace. See Section 5.4.

Audio Settings Modal (AUDIO SETTINGS)#

Hardware configuration dialog opened via AUDIO SETTINGS in the Top Deck (CMD + , on macOS, CTRL + , on Windows and Linux). Selects the physical input and output audio interface, sample rate (44.1, 48.0, 88.2, or 96.0 kHz), hardware buffer size (32 to 2048 frames), and runs the automated hardware impulse loopback latency calibration (Measure Latency). See Section 2.2 and Section 2.3.

Aux Return (AUX 1 REVERB & AUX 2 TAPE DLY)#

Two dedicated, solo-safe stereo effect return strips on the right side of the Mix Console feeding the Master Bus:

  • AUX 1 REVERB: Zero-latency acoustic convolution reverb (EMT 140 Plate, Studio Room, Chamber, Spring) with Input Low-Cut (LC), Pre-Delay (PD, 0 to 200 ms), Decay Time (DCY, 0.20 to 12.00 s), and WET blend toggle.
  • AUX 2 TAPE DLY: Capstan magnetic tape echo processor with Wow & Flutter (WOW), High-Frequency Damping (DMP), Delay Time (TM, 1 to 2000 ms), Feedback (FB), and Stereo Ping-Pong (PP) widening.

See Section 4.3.2 and Section 5.5.

Aux Send (RVB & DLY / PRE, EQ, PST)#

Per-channel and per-subgroup stereo send level controls (-inf to +12.0 dB) feeding AUX 1 REVERB (RVB) and AUX 2 TAPE DLY (DLY), paired with a 3-way pickoff tap selector: PRE (pre-insert and pre-fader for independent cue sends), EQ (post-EQ and pre-fader), or PST (post-fader and post-mute, scaling wet effects naturally with fader moves). See Section 4.2.3.

Base Pass (Rec001 / PASS 01)#

The initial multitrack audio stem captured on a record-armed channel strip when a Session Take is originally recorded from 00:00:00.000 with Overdub Mode (OD) turned off. Stored on disk in <TakeFolder>/rec/<SessionID>_Take<NNN>_<CC>-<TrackName>_Rec001_<Stamp>.wav and displayed as PASS 01 (BASE PASS) in the channel strip header and Pass Lanes. See Section 1.2 and Section 6.2.

Channel Inspector (CHANNEL INSPECTOR)#

Full-screen single-channel and Subgroup Bus processing workbench (CMD + 3 on macOS, CTRL + 3 on Windows and Linux). Combines a full-height channel strip on the left with an expanded 12-band Parametric EQ and Dual-Trace 4096-point RTA, an expanded Analog VCA Compressor with scrolling Gain Reduction trace and dedicated rotary knobs, a multi-bus output routing popover (OUTPUT BUSES), and bottom Pass Lane comping controls. See Section 5.1.2.

CLAP Plugin (1:CLAP)#

Third-party audio effect plugin conforming to the open CLAP (Clever Audio Plug-in, .clap) standard, hostable in any of the eight reorderable slots (Slots 1..8) on any Input Track or Subgroup Bus with native floating GUI support, automatic Plugin Delay Compensation (PDC), and one-click Low-Latency Monitoring bypass. See Section 5.7.

Cloud Sync (CLOUD)#

Built-in two-way Google Drive synchronization engine for backing up Sessions and collaborating across bandmates' computers. Supports two transfer tiers (MIXES for fast synchronization of manifests, Snapshots, and .flac Prints/Mixdowns, and FULL for complete raw rec/*.wav multitrack stems), automatic Print-to-FLAC conversion (AUTO-FLAC ON UPLOAD), federated shared project linking (+ Link Remote Project...), exclusive session write-locking (Read-Only Preview), and zero-loss offline conflict merging via Snapshots. See Section 7.5.

Cycle Loop (⇄)#

Master Transport mode (C or clicking ⇄ in the Top Deck) that loops playback or overdub recording continuously between the START and END boundaries of the active Take Range (R001, R003, ...). See Section 3.4 and Section 6.3.2.

Dual-Trace Spectrum Analyzer (RTA)#

High-resolution 4096-point real-time acoustic spectrum analyzer displayed behind the 12-Band Parametric EQ curve in both the Mix Console Upper Well 2 and the CHANNEL INSPECTOR view. Plots the pre-EQ input spectrum (darker shaded trace) and post-EQ output spectrum (bright emerald trace) simultaneously from 20 Hz to 20 kHz. See Section 5.3.2.

Input Monitor (I)#

Per-track console switch (I on channel strips, MON in Channel Inspector, shortcut SHIFT + I on the inspected track in Mix Console) that routes the live physical hardware input (IN 01..IN 18) through the channel's 8-slot processor rack and fader even while transport playback is rolling, allowing musicians to rehearse or monitor live overdubs through console EQ, compression, and reverb. See Section 4.2.4.

Input Trim (TRM)#

Digital pre-insert gain control (-24.0 dB to +24.0 dB, default 0.0 dB) located at the top of every Input Track and Subgroup Bus strip. On Input Tracks, TRM sits upstream of both the raw multitrack stem recorder (rec/) and the 8-slot processor rack so you can fine-tune preamp gain staging before stems hit disk. On Subgroup Buses, TRM gain-stages the summed multi-channel signal entering the subgroup's SUB RACK. See Section 4.1 and Section 4.2.2.

Keymap View (KEYMAP)#

Interactive keyboard shortcut reference and customization workspace (CMD + 6 on macOS, CTRL + 6 on Windows and Linux). Lists all 58 studio actions across seven scopes with live substring search, interactive Reverse-Search by Shortcut (FIND BY SHORTCUT:), scope filter pills, inline key-chord capture (ASSIGN / APPLY), scope clash detection, operating-system conflict warnings (OS WARN), and automatic persistence to keymap.json. See Section 8.3.

Live Stereo Print (2-TRK / Print001)#

Real-time 2-track post-Master-Bus stereo recording captured concurrently alongside raw pre-fader multitrack stems whenever a Take is recorded with 2-TRK enabled on the MST MASTER BUS strip. Written to <TakeFolder>/mix/<SessionID>_Take<NNN>_Print<NNN>_<Stamp>.wav and convertible in one click (FLAC PRINT or FLAC ALL PRINTS) to a lossless 24-bit .flac file linked into <ProjectTag>/_Mixes/ for immediate post-rehearsal band listening. See Section 1.1.3, Section 4.1.1, and Section 7.2.

Low-Latency Monitoring#

Top Deck monitoring mode (Low-Latency Monitoring in Row 2 of the Top Deck) that temporarily bypasses high-latency lookahead CLAP plugins on record-armed (R) live monitoring paths and their downstream buses during overdubbing, dropping foldback latency to zero internal samples while keeping full Plugin Delay Compensation active on unarmed playback tracks. See Section 5.7.3.

Markers & Ranges (M001 / R001)#

Sample-accurate timeline cue flags and bounded time spans stored per Take and managed in both the WAVEFORM TIMELINE (MKR and RNG lanes) and the dedicated MARKERS & RANGES workspace (CMD/CTRL + 5). Standalone cue markers (M005, ...) are dropped on the fly with M and navigated via [, ], SHIFT + [ (HOME), and SHIFT + ] (END). Ranges (R001, R003, ...) are bounded by start (K) and end (L) markers for Cycle Loop playback (⇄) and non-destructive Take extraction (NEW TAKE). See Section 6.3.

Master Bus (MST MASTER BUS)#

The final stereo summing strip on the right edge of the Mix Console, summing all Subgroup Buses, direct Input Tracks, and Stereo Aux Returns. Incorporates a built-in Master Tape Saturation stage (TAPE with DRIVE and WARM), a Master Bus Glue VCA Compressor (COMP), a Lookahead Brickwall Limiter (LIM with THR and CEIL), the live 2-TRK Print recorder arm switch, a one-click FLAC offline Mixdown bounce button, CLR CLIP, Master Fader, and 40-segment stereo Peak/RMS meter. See Section 4.3.3.

Mix Console (MIX CONSOLE)#

Primary studio workspace view (CMD + 1 on macOS, CTRL + 1 on Windows and Linux) combining the four Upper Inspector Wells (8-Slot Rack & Tape, 12-Band EQ & RTA, VCA Compressor & GR, and Mini-Timeline) with the lower bank of 18 Input Track strips (CH01..CH18), stereo Subgroup Buses (DRUM BUS, INST BUS), Stereo Aux Returns (AUX 1 REVERB, AUX 2 TAPE DLY), and the MST MASTER BUS. See Chapter 4.

Multi-Mic Phase Alignment (ALIGN ALL & Φ)#

Automatic acoustic arrival-time and polarity correlation processor that analyzes phase relationships across multi-miked instruments (such as a drum kit or dual-miked guitar/bass cabinet) relative to a timing anchor channel (0us). Clicking ALIGN ALL in the Top Deck (or Φ on an individual strip) calculates and engages sub-sample time delay (0 to 50,000 us / 0.00 to +50.00 ms) and 180-degree polarity inversion (Ø) to eliminate comb filtering, with instant global comparison via PHASE: ALIGN / PHASE: BYP. See Section 3.3 and Section 5.6.

Offline Stereo Mixdown (Mix001)#

Faster-than-real-time stereo render of an entire Take processed from 00:00:00.000 through the complete Mix Console signal chain (including updated fader balances, 8-slot inserts, Subgroup/Aux buses, Master Bus processing, and active Pass Lane selections). Triggered via the FLAC button on the Master Bus or from the SESSION REEL view, saved to <TakeFolder>/mix/<SessionID>_Take<NNN>_Mix<NNN>_<Stamp>.flac (or .wav), and linked into <ProjectTag>/_Mixes/. See Section 7.2.3.

Overdub Mode (OD)#

Transport recording mode toggled via the red OD button in the Top Deck (or pressing O). When OD is off, pressing Record (R) creates a brand-new t = 0 Session Take. When OD is latched (illuminated red), pressing Record (R) plays back the existing tracks of the currently active Take while recording a new track-level Overdub Pass (Rec002-OD, OD 02) onto armed channels with automatic roundtrip loopback latency compensation. See Section 3.4 and Section 6.2.

Overdub Pass (Rec002-OD / OD 02)#

A layered or replacement track-level recording captured inside an existing Session Take when Overdub Mode (OD) is enabled on the transport bar. Saved as <TakeFolder>/rec/<SessionID>_Take<NNN>_<CC>-<TrackName>_Rec<MMM>-OD_<Stamp>.wav and displayed as OD 02, OD 03, and higher inside that track's expandable Pass Lanes. See Section 1.2 and Section 6.2.

Pass#

Any individual audio recording on a single channel strip belonging to a specific Session Take, encompassing both the initial Base Pass (Rec001 / PASS 01) captured when the Take was first created and any subsequent Overdub Passes (Rec002-OD / OD 02, OD 03, ...) recorded into that Take. See Section 9.1.

Pass Lanes (PASSES)#

Expandable vertical sub-lanes under an Input Track in WAVEFORM TIMELINE (CMD/CTRL + 2, expanded with E and collapsed with SHIFT + E) and CHANNEL INSPECTOR (CMD/CTRL + 3, toggled with E or PASSES). Pass Lanes display the track's PASS 01 Base Pass and all OD 02..N Overdub Passes inside the active Take for one-click comp promotion (Use PASS 01 / ★ OD 02), per-pass muting (M), and non-destructive deletion (Del). Track-level overdub lanes are always called Pass Lanes and are never called "Take Lanes". See Section 6.2.2.

Plugin Delay Compensation (PDC)#

Automatic sample-accurate delay compensation engine that aligns parallel tracks, Subgroup Buses, and Aux paths whenever a third-party CLAP plugin introduces internal lookahead buffering. Displayed live in Row 2 of the Top Deck (PDC: N smp (X.XX ms @ 48 kHz)) and detailed per channel in the Latency & PDC Transparency Breakdown popover. See Section 5.7.2.

Polarity Invert (Ø)#

Console switch (Ø, shortcut SHIFT + P on the inspected strip in Mix Console) that flips positive and negative waveform excursions by 180° (illuminates cyan when active). Used to correct phase cancellation on bottom snare microphones, rear open-back guitar cabinet microphones, or inverted balanced XLR cables, and set automatically during ALIGN ALL. See Section 4.2.2 and Section 5.6.

Present Stash (A/B PRESENT STASH & RETURN TO PRESENT)#

Automatic working-memory safeguard that preserves your live, unsaved Mix Console state the instant you click AUDITION SNAP <NNN> in the SESSION REEL view (CMD/CTRL + 4). While in Audition Mode, you can step across any number of historical Snapshots, click A/B PRESENT STASH for clickless real-time comparison against your working mix, or press BACKSPACE (RETURN TO PRESENT) to restore your exact working console state with zero lost fader moves. See Section 7.3.2.

Project & Project Tag (PROJECT / _Mixes/)#

Top-level workspace representing a band, artist, or musical venture, identified by a unique 1-to-8 character uppercase alphanumeric Project Tag (GF, TEST, MISC) and stored at <LibraryRoot>/<ProjectTag>/. Managed in the PROJECT modal (CMD/CTRL + O), each Project contains all of that band's Sessions plus a flat _Mixes/ index folder containing relative links to all active .flac Prints and Mixdowns across every Session. See Section 1.2, Section 2.4, and Section 7.4.

Roundtrip Loopback Calibration (Measure Latency)#

Built-in hardware impulse measurement tool inside the AUDIO SETTINGS modal (CMD/CTRL + ,) that transmits a calibration pulse out a selected physical output jack (Out 20 or Out 1) over a patch cable into an analog input jack to measure exact physical converter, ADAT, and USB/Thunderbolt roundtrip delay in samples. The measured offset is automatically applied as negative record-offset compensation on all subsequent Overdub Passes (OD). See Section 2.3.

Session & 08:00 Rollover Rule#

A single rehearsal evening, jam night, or studio tracking day within a Project (<ProjectTag>/<SessionID>[_<SessionTag>]/), defining the shared 18-channel hardware patch, strip names, bus topology, and console settings across all Takes recorded during that gathering. Cave Recorder rolls the session calendar date over at 08:00 local time so Takes recorded after midnight (01:30) remain grouped under the same evening session date (YYYYMMDD) as recordings started at 21:00. See Section 1.2 and Section 7.4.2.

Session Reel (SESSION REEL)#

Session management dashboard (CMD + 4 on macOS, CTRL + 4 on Windows and Linux) containing the session metadata and cloud sync header, the reversible Undo/Redo history bar (UNDO, REDO, COMPACT TAIL, SNAP MANUAL), the searchable TAKES CATALOG (Take loading, starring, tagging, FLAC PRINT encoding, soft-delete trash management, and latency offset re-application), and the SNAPSHOTS & PRESENT STASH table. See Chapter 7.

Session Template (SAVE TEMPLATE)#

Reusable console configuration file (<LibraryRoot>/.caverec/templates/<Name>.json) saved via the SAVE TEMPLATE button in Row 2 of the Top Deck and selectable when creating a new Session in the PROJECT modal. Stores all channel strip names, physical input bindings, Subgroup Bus assignments, 8-slot processor racks, EQ curves, and compressor settings while excluding recorded audio Takes and undo history. See Section 2.4.3.

Snapshot (SNAPS)#

Immutable point-in-time capture of the entire Mix Console state, active Pass Lane selections, and compact undo history stored in <SessionFolder>/snapshots/<SessionID>_<NNN>_<Timestamp>.json. Created manually via SNAP MANUAL (CMD/CTRL + S), automatically after every normal recording stop ([POST-REC]), or every 20 minutes during active editing ([AUTO]), and auditioned losslessly via Present Stash. See Section 7.3.

Standby Comp Lane (STANDBY COMP LANE)#

Any recorded Pass Lane (PASS 01 Base Pass or OD 02..N Overdub Pass) on a track that is not currently selected as the ACTIVE COMP LANE. Rendered in slate-grey waveform color with a clickable Use PASS <NN> or Use OD <NN> button that promotes the standby pass to active playback immediately without audio clicks. See Section 6.2.2.

Stem Bounce (Bounce001)#

Faster-than-real-time offline post-processor render of individual Input Track channels (<CC>-<TrackName>_Bounce<NNN>.wav / .flac) and/or stereo Subgroup Buses (Bus<BB>-<BusName>_Bounce<NNN>.wav / .flac) written into <TakeFolder>/bounce/. Bounced stems start at exact sample 0 (00:00:00.000) with all insert processing, phase alignment, and Plugin Delay Compensation applied for delivery to external remixing or mastering engineers. See Section 7.2.3.

Subgroup Bus (SUB 1 DRUM BUS & SUB 2 INST BUS)#

Stereo summing bus strip (SUB 1 through SUB 16) that combines routed Input Tracks into a shared stereo processing chain with its own Input Trim (TRM), Stereo Link (LINK), SEL inspection button, reorderable 8-Slot SUB RACK (Tape Saturation, 12-Band EQ with Dual-Trace RTA, Analog VCA Bus Compressor, and CLAP plugins), stereo Aux Sends (RVB and DLY), stereo Balance (BAL), Mute (M), Solo (S), and logarithmic stereo fader. See Section 4.3.1.

Take (TAKE 001)#

A complete, self-contained multitrack song recording or rehearsal jam captured within a Session (<SessionFolder>/Take<NNN>_<Stamp>[_<TakeTag>]/), starting at its own independent timeline origin (00:00:00.000, sample 0). Pressing Record (R) with Overdub Mode (OD) off always creates a new numbered Take (Take 001, Take 002, ...) and switches the active timeline to that Take. Two different Takes are never stacked on top of one another in the same timeline view. See Section 1.1.1 and Section 9.1.

Take Splitting (SPLIT TAKE & NEW TAKE)#

Non-destructive editing operations in WAVEFORM TIMELINE (CMD/CTRL + 2) and MARKERS & RANGES (CMD/CTRL + 5) that divide a continuous recording at the Playhead (SPLIT TAKE), extract from a standalone marker to the end (NEW TAKE -> END), or extract a bounded range (NEW TAKE) into a brand-new t = 0 Session Take while preserving all original .wav files, Pass Lanes, and phase alignment offsets with full Undo support. See Section 6.4.

Tape Saturation (TAPE)#

Zero-latency analog magnetic tape modeling stage built into every Input Track, Subgroup Bus, and the Master Bus. Models transformer core warmth, magnetic hysteresis soft-clipping, AC bias asymmetry, and high-frequency head self-erasure via six continuous controls: Input Drive (DRV), Harmonic Warmth (WRM), Tape Bias (BIAS), High-Cut Roll-Off (HC), Output Trim (OUT), and Parallel Wet/Dry Mix (MIX). See Section 5.2.

Waveform Timeline (WAVEFORM TIMELINE)#

Multi-track waveform editing and overdub comping workspace (CMD + 2 on macOS, CTRL + 2 on Windows and Linux) focused on the currently active t = 0 Session Take. Features three-tier waveform detail (3-TIER LOD), one-handed 2D ruler scrub-zooming, expandable Pass Lanes (PASSES, E / SHIFT + E), dedicated Marker (MKR) and Range (RNG) lanes, Playhead/Cursor lock (LOCK PH/CUR), and non-destructive Take splitting (SPLIT TAKE / NEW TAKE). See Chapter 6.
