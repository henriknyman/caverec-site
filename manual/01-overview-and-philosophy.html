Overview & Studio Philosophy

The Human Musicianship Manifesto, the Anti-Bloat Curated Console Philosophy, independent t=0 Takes, 30-minute retroactive memory, live stereo Prints, and track-level Overdub Passes.

1.1 Why Cave Recorder Exists: The Human Musicianship Manifesto#

Cave Recorder (caverec) is a multitrack studio recorder, live mixing console, automatic phase-alignment processor, and band collaboration workstation purpose-built for rehearsal rooms, live tracking spaces, and project studios.

Modern music production has drifted toward sterile isolation. In an era of AI-generated tracks and hyper-commercial pop production, performances are routinely recorded one person at a time to a rigid click track, snapped to a mathematical bar/beat grid, and stripped of pitch and timing micro-variations. That pipeline works for synthetic programmed audio, but it destroys the soul of music that depends on human interaction in a shared room.

Across jazz, folk, stoner rock, doom, psychedelic and space rock, krautrock, blues, soul, and punk, the magic of a great record lives in the unrepeatable human moment: the shared eye contact before a dynamic breakdown, the collective tempo push of a rhythm section leaning into a chorus, the natural acoustic bleed of drums and cranked amplifiers breathing in the same room, and the spontaneous twenty-minute jam that nobody planned.

Cave Recorder takes a clear philosophical stand:

  1. Human Musicianship First, Zero Grid or Pitch-Correction Bloat: Cave Recorder intentionally omits MIDI sequencers, piano rolls, beat grids, tempo-warping elastic audio, and robotic vocal pitch correction. Time is measured in real physical seconds, milliseconds, and sample frames (00:00:00.000) so the band's natural tempo fluctuations and groove breathe without a visual metronome dictating the performance.
  2. Technology as an Invisible Studio Assistant, Never a Replacement: Historically, capturing a full band live in one room with the punch and clarity of classic analog studio records required a dedicated recording engineer, an isolated control room, and two days spent moving microphone stands by millimeters to eliminate phase cancellation and amplifier buzz. Self-recording bands in rehearsal caves and basements rarely have a second person at the desk or hours to spend on technical setup. Cave Recorder acts as an invisible studio engineer that handles rolling 30-minute memory capture, one-click -18 dBFS gain staging, multicore spectral phase and polarity alignment, bleed-aware drum and amplifier gating, and non-destructive mistake surgery automatically. Musicians stay focused on their instruments and each other.
  3. Curated Analog-Inspired Console Instead of 90 Confusing Plugins: General-purpose workstations overwhelm musicians with dozens of overlapping legacy plugins, floating windows, and arcane parameter matrices. Cave Recorder provides a single, deeply integrated studio console and rack suite where every processor opens on a musical sweet spot for immediate results, while exposing deep physical controls underneath when an experienced mix engineer wants surgical precision.

Traditional digital audio workstations are modeled around a single linear timeline designed for solo production at a desk. When a full band plugs into an 18-channel audio interface for a three-hour rehearsal or live tracking session, a single continuous timeline creates immediate studio bottlenecks: finding a specific song run-through requires scrolling through hours of waveforms, spontaneous jams are lost when nobody remembers to press Record, bandmates wait days for someone to bounce rough mixes, and multi-microphone phase cancellation hollows out the drum kit and bass rig.

Cave Recorder solves four foundational studio and rehearsal problems:

1.1.1 Independent Zero-Based Takes Instead of a 3-Hour Linear Timeline#

In a band rehearsal or tracking session, musicians play multiple run-throughs of songs alongside spontaneous improvisations. In Cave Recorder, every time you press Record (R) with Overdub Mode turned off, the transport creates a brand-new, self-contained Take (Take 001, Take 002, Take 003, ...) that starts at its own zeroed timeline origin (00:00:00.000, sample 0).

Two different Takes are never appended end-to-end down a multi-hour timeline and are never stacked on top of one another in the same timeline view. Instead, switching the active Take in the Top Transport Deck (< TAKE 001 >) or via ALT + [ and ALT + ] immediately switches the Waveform Timeline and Mix Console playback to that song's 00:00:00.000 origin while keeping the room's microphone assignments, channel names, subgroup routing, and console mix intact.

1.1.2 30-Minute Retroactive Capture Memory (RAM & RETRO)#

The best moments in a rehearsal room often happen when the transport is stopped while the band is warming up or exploring a groove between songs. Whenever the audio interface is active and the green RAM indicator is lit in the Top Transport Deck, Cave Recorder continuously retains the last 30 minutes of live multi-channel audio across all record-armed channels in dedicated system memory.

Pressing the RETRO button in the Top Deck (or pressing SHIFT + R) immediately rescues the rolling 30-minute capture memory to disk as a new numbered Take (Take<NNN>), complete with individual pre-fader multitrack stems and a reference stereo file.

1.1.3 Simultaneous Live Console Print and Raw Multitrack Stems#

During every Take, Cave Recorder writes two distinct sets of audio files to disk in real time:

  1. Raw Pre-Fader Multitrack Stems (rec/): Every record-armed input channel (CH01 through CH18) writes a discrete, uncompressed 32-bit floating-point mono .wav stem tapped directly after input gain trim and prior to channel insert processing and fader moves. This guarantees clip-free archival stems for later mixing or overdubbing.
  2. Live Stereo Master Bus Print (mix/): Simultaneously, when 2-TRK is armed on the MST MASTER BUS, a dedicated 2-track recorder tapped at the post-limiter output of the Master Bus captures the live stereo console mix (Print001.wav) with automatic Plugin Delay Compensation (PDC) latency trimming, including all channel EQ, VCA compression, tape saturation, subgroup processing, and auxiliary reverb and delay returns.

With a single click on FLAC PRINT or MP3 PRINT in the Session Reel (or automatically upon cloud upload when AUTO-FLAC ON UPLOAD or AUTO-MP3 ON UPLOAD is enabled), Cave Recorder encodes the live stereo Print to 24-bit lossless .flac and/or 320 kbps CBR .mp3 (with a Xing/Info seek header for direct playback inside the Google Drive web browser player) and links it into the project-wide _Mixes/ folder. Band members walk out of rehearsal with finished stereo reference mixes ready for phone, browser, or car playback, while the engineer retains pristine 32-bit floating-point raw stems.

1.1.4 Console-First Layout and Automatic Multi-Mic Phase & Polarity Alignment#

Cave Recorder eliminates nested plugin windows and hidden routing matrices in favor of a fixed, dark-chassis studio console interface. Input gain trim with one-click CALIBRATE ALL TRIMS, polarity inversion (Ø), per-channel sub-sample delay (Φ) paired with a zero-slot 5-stage allpass PhaseAlignNetwork, an 8-slot reorderable processor rack (12-band parametric Dynamic EQ with dual-trace spectrum analyzer, multi-topology VCA/FET/Opto compressor, bleed- and harmonicity-aware noise gate, vocal rider, de-esser, transient shaper, micro-pitch doubler, 3-mode delay, 4-pole resonant ladder filter, studio plate reverb, 4-mode modulation, tape saturation, CLAP .clap plugins, and native macOS AUv3/AUv2 Audio Unit effects), stereo Aux Sends (RVB and DLY), Subgroup Bus routing (SUB 1..16), and 100 mm logarithmic faders are always accessible directly on the console faceplate.

When recording a drum kit or multi-miked guitar and bass cabinets, acoustic arrival-time differences and cabinet filter dispersion between close microphones, overheads, DI boxes, and room microphones cause comb filtering and 6 to 12 dB of low-frequency cancellation. Clicking ALIGN ALL in the Top Deck aligns acoustic arrival times to sub-sample accuracy (us / smp), while the two-tier Polarity & Phase Maximizer solves both instant broadband polarity inversions (Mode 1, completing in under 50 ms) and deep multicore frequency-dependent allpass phase rotation (Mode 2, 30 s to 30 min anytime solver with loudness-matched Candidates A, B, C, D auditioning) without moving a single microphone stand.

Distinguishing Session-Level Takes from Track-Level Passes

Cave Recorder maintains a strict separation between Takes and Passes. A Take (Take 001, Take 002) is an entire song or jam recorded across the studio room starting at 00:00:00.000, selected in the Top Transport Deck or SESSION REEL (CMD + 4 / CTRL + 4). A Pass (PASS 01 Base Pass or OD 02 Overdub Pass) is an individual performance recorded on a single channel strip inside the currently active Take, displayed inside expandable Pass Lanes in WAVEFORM TIMELINE (CMD + 2 / CTRL + 2).

1.3 Tour of the Studio Workspace#

Cave Recorder opens into a dark studio console workspace divided into three horizontal tiers: the persistent Top Transport & Session Header Deck, the Upper Inspector Wells, and the Lower Console Channel & Bus Strips.

  1. Top Transport & Session Header Deck (Top): Remains pinned at the top of the screen across all seven workspace views (MIX CONSOLE through PLUGINS and KEYMAP). Houses project and session selection, Google Drive cloud sync status, 30-minute retroactive memory controls, main transport buttons, the active Take selector, high-precision digital timecode and sample counters, global multi-mic phase alignment controls, and latency diagnostics.
  2. Upper Inspector Wells (Middle): Displays four side-by-side real-time visualizers and editors for the currently selected channel strip or Subgroup Bus:
    • 8-Slot Reorderable Processor Rack & Tape Controls (Left Well): Shows slots 1 through 8 (1:CLAP, 2:12B EQ, 3:VCA CMP, 4:AUX SND, ...), per-slot bypass (B), up/down slot reordering arrows, a dedicated TAPE saturator enable/bypass toggle with analog tape parameters (DRV, WRM, BIAS, HC, OUT, MIX), SWAP EQ/CMP, and channel RESET.
    • 12-Band Parametric EQ & Dual-Trace RTA Spectrum Analyzer (Center-Left Well): Interactive Bode magnitude curve with draggable filter nodes (BELL, LSHF, HSHF, HPF, TILT) superimposed over a pre-EQ and post-EQ real-time spectrum trace (20 Hz to 20 kHz).
    • VCA Compressor & Gain Reduction Transfer Curve (Center-Right Well): Displays the compressor knee and transfer curve (-48 dB to 0 dB), input/output threshold markers, sidechain high-pass filter (SC HPF), ratio presets (2:1, 4:1, 8:1), parallel blend (MIX 50%), and real-time Gain Reduction (GR) metering.
    • Upper Mini-Timeline & Multi-Tier Waveform Overview (Right Well): Displays the composite waveform of the active Take with playhead tracking, PASSES expansion shortcut, LOCK PH/CUR, -> CUR, and zoom controls (ZOOM +, ZOOM -, ZOOM 1:1).
  3. Lower Console Strips (Bottom): Houses up to 18 Input Channel Strips (CH01..CH18), stereo Subgroup Bus Strips (DRUM BUS, INST BUS), dedicated stereo Aux Return Strips (AUX 1 REVERB with zero-latency convolution IR and AUX 2 TAPE DLY with capstan tape echo), and the MST MASTER BUS strip with Master Tape, Master Compressor, brickwall Limiter, live 2-TRK Print record arm, independent FLAC and MP3 format toggles, and one-click BOUNCE offline Mixdown render controls.

1.4 The Anti-Bloat Curated Console Philosophy#

General-purpose workstations and large commercial plugin bundles often ship with 70 to 90 separate effects plugins: five nearly identical equalizers, eight redundant compressors named after vintage circuit board revisions, four separate de-essers, and dozens of single-purpose utility modules. In a fast-moving band rehearsal or self-engineered tracking session, scrolling through huge plugin menus and juggling floating windows breaks musical momentum and causes decision fatigue.

Cave Recorder replaces plugin sprawl with a Curated Native Console & Rack Suite built around five engineering pillars:

1.4.1 Pillar 1: Fewer, Deeper Multi-Mode Instruments Instead of Redundant Clones#

Rather than cluttering the rack with dozens of single-purpose plugins, Cave Recorder consolidates classic studio hardware topologies into focused, multi-mode processors:

  • One 3-Topology Compressor (VCA, FET, Opto): Switches cleanly between feed-forward RMS bus glue (VCA), ultra-fast discrete transistor transient limiting with odd-harmonic grit (FET), and program-dependent dual-stage electroluminescent optical leveling (Opto).
  • One Context-Aware 3-Variant Gate (Expander, DrumBleed, AmpDynamic): Combines a smooth downward vocal/room expander, a differential sidechain tom/snare bleed gate with automatic fundamental lock ([LRN]), and a pitch-harmonicity and spectral-entropy amplifier gate built for high-gain stoner, doom, and psychedelic guitar rigs.
  • One 3-Mode Echo & Delay (Capstan Tape, BBD Analog, Pristine Digital): Covers saturating magnetic tape echo, dark bucket-brigade analog delay with compander breathe and clock noise, and clean stereo rhythmic delay with dynamic ducking.
  • One 4-Mode Studio Modulator (ThroughZeroFlanger, DimensionChorus, OpticalPhaser, HarmonicTremolo): Covers tape-style through-zero flanging, motionless stereo dimension widening, 4/6/8/12-stage photocell phasing, and dual-band crossover harmonic tremolo.

1.4.2 Pillar 2: Immediate Musical Sweet Spots with Deep Custom Power Underneath#

Every native processor in Cave Recorder is tuned so that engaging it on a channel strip immediately improves the sound without requiring five minutes of parameter tweaking:

  • One-Click Calibration & Auto-Setup: Clicking CALIBRATE ALL TRIMS sets every channel's input gain to the -18 dBFS RMS console sweet spot with a -6 dBFS peak ceiling. Clicking [LRN] on the DrumBleed gate listens to a single tom hit and automatically locks the resonant key filter to that drum's fundamental pitch (55 Hz to 350 Hz, default 110 Hz). Engaging VocalRider with Breath / Bleed Freeze holds the fader steady during pauses so drum bleed is never boosted between vocal lines.
  • Full Surgical Depth When Needed: Beneath these immediate defaults, mix engineers have full access to per-band Dynamic EQ thresholds, 16-sample smoothed filter trajectories, sidechain Tilt EQ, C² quintic gate ballistics, 5-stage allpass frequency/Q coordinates, and loudness-matched AUTO-LVL bypass auditioning.

1.4.3 Pillar 3: Solving Real Live-Room Physics (Phase Dispersion, Bleed & High-Gain Sustain)#

Plugins designed for isolated bedroom overdubs fall apart when an entire band plays loud in an untreated room. Cave Recorder's processors are engineered specifically for the acoustic realities of live band tracking:

  • Multi-Mic Phase Cancellation & Allpass Dispersion: Combining a bass DI with a miked ported bass cabinet, or blending 8 microphones across a drum kit, introduces both acoustic time-of-flight delays and frequency-dependent phase rotation across cabinet ports and crossover networks. Every channel strip includes a zero-slot top-of-strip PhaseAlignNetwork driven by the two-mode Polarity & Phase Maximizer (Mode 1 instant binary polarity solver in under 50 ms, and Mode 2 multicore spectral allpass solver).
  • Snare-into-Tom Spill & Cymbal Wash: The DrumBleed gate uses a differential key detector (comparing the target tom against a snare or overhead reference) and a dynamic decay low-pass filter (20 kHz sweeping down to 250 Hz to 800 Hz, default 380 Hz) so tom hits ring out naturally without sucking bursts of hi-hat and cymbal wash into the mix.
  • Stoner, Doom & Psychedelic High-Gain Guitar Dynamics: Standard threshold noise gates destroy quiet clean fingerpicking (-34 dBFS) and chop off intentional harmonic amplifier feedback bloom, or else sit wide open while stacked fuzz pedals roar at -22 dBFS. The AmpDynamic gate measures 8x-decimated autocorrelation pitch periodicity (H ∈ [0, 1]) and spectral flatness so whispered clean passages and 1.5 s to 5.0 s feedback sustain pass untouched while 60 Hz hum and fuzz hiss clamp shut in 15 ms to 40 ms (default 18 ms to 22 ms) on a palm-mute stop.

1.4.4 Pillar 4: Non-Destructive Live-Take Salvage and Mistake Surgery#

When a band captures a once-in-a-lifetime live groove, you cannot ask them to play the exact same improvisation again just because a snare rimshot clipped an preamp converter for 12 samples, a vintage combo amp buzzed at 60 Hz, or the guitarist hit a wrong chord in bar 42.

Cave Recorder treats live-take rescue as a core console workflow rather than an external audio restoration chore. Directly inside the Waveform Timeline and Channel Inspector, you can reconstruct clipped ADC peaks ([REPAIR CLIPPING] using cubic Hermite splines and 16th-order Burg autoregressive linear prediction), notch out 12 harmonics of 50/60 Hz power-line hum without dulling guitar tone, comp a single bar from an Overdub Pass ([USE OD 02 IN RANGE]), graft a clean chord from another bar ([PATCH FROM SOURCE...]), or apply a spectral spot-heal ([SMOOTH OVER ARTIFACT]) while leaving the original 32-bit floating-point recording on disk completely untouched.

1.4.5 Pillar 5: Strict Separation Between Master Bus Printing and Control Room Monitoring#

In a rehearsal or tracking room, engineers constantly need to check mono fold-down compatibility (MONO), solo the stereo side difference (DIFF), isolate the sub-bass (SUB < 90 Hz) or midrange (MID 350 Hz - 3.5 kHz) across studio monitors, dim the speakers (DIM -20 dB) to talk to the band, or A/B a heavy processing chain at matched loudness (AUTO-LVL).

In Cave Recorder, the Control Room Monitor Bar is wired strictly downstream of the Master Bus 2-TRK live Print tap and offline BOUNCE engine. You can solo frequency bands, flip to mono, or dim the control room speakers right in the middle of recording a live Take without ever altering or interrupting the stereo Print001.wav being written for the band.
